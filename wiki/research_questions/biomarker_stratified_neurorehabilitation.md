# Biomarker-Stratified Neurorehabilitation

## Question

How should baseline brain health, systemic health, sensory function, imaging markers, and network metrics be used to stratify neurorehabilitation studies and interpret treatment response without overclaiming causality or excluding high-need patients?

## Source-Backed Rationale

- `2026-06-03_fridriksson_brain-health-aphasia-recovery`: Lecture notes argue that aphasia recovery is shaped by upstream drivers such as hypertension, diabetes, glycemic control, and hearing loss; structural brain-health axes such as Brain Age Gap and white matter hyperintensities; network mechanisms such as structural disconnection and controllability; and the stroke lesion pathway.

## Open Subquestions

- Which brain-health and systemic-health variables should be routinely captured at rehabilitation intake?
- How should Brain Age Gap, white matter hyperintensity burden, Fazekas scores, and lesion characteristics be combined without creating opaque or overfit prediction models?
- When should biomarker profiles be used for trial stratification, covariate adjustment, subgroup analysis, or intervention tailoring?
- How do diabetes, blood pressure, hearing loss, and other upstream drivers alter rehabilitation response across acute, subacute, and chronic phases?
- What longitudinal imaging and behavioral data are needed to distinguish statistical mediation from causal recovery mechanisms?
- How can stratification improve trial interpretability without reducing access for people with more complex or less favorable baseline brain-health profiles?
