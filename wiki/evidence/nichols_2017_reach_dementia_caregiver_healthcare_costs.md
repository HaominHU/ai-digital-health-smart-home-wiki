# Nichols 2017 Evidence on REACH Dementia Caregiver Intervention Healthcare Costs

## Source Role

This article is useful as implementation and economic evidence for dementia caregiver behavioral interventions. It links REACH II and REACH VA caregiver intervention participation with care-recipient and caregiver healthcare cost data where available.

## Full-Study Picture

- Problem/gap: Prior REACH work showed dementia caregiver benefits, but health systems may hesitate to adopt caregiver interventions if they appear likely to increase patient or caregiver healthcare costs.
- Population/condition/setting: Caregivers and care recipients with Alzheimer disease and related dementias. REACH II drew from five community sites; REACH VA drew from 24 VA facilities and Home Based Primary Care context.
- Intervention/phenomenon: REACH II was an RCT caregiver behavioral intervention. REACH VA was a VA translation implemented as usual clinical care with a matched comparison group.
- Methods/sample/measures: REACH II cost analysis used Medicare expenditures for an analysis sample of 110 caregivers and 197 care recipients. REACH VA used VA costs plus Medicare expenditures for care recipients in an intervention-versus-propensity-matched comparison.
- Key results: Neither REACH II nor REACH VA was associated with increased VA or Medicare expenditures for care recipients or caregivers where data were available. REACH VA was associated with 33.6% lower total VA care costs for care recipients. VA caregiver cost data were not available.
- Primary takeaway: Dementia caregiver behavioral interventions can be framed as support mechanisms that may avoid additional healthcare costs, and in an integrated VA setting may be associated with lower care-recipient costs.

## Wiki Interpretation

Use this source for caregiver intervention implementation, payer, and economic evaluation logic. It supports adding healthcare utilization and cost outcomes to caregiver intervention evaluation, especially when interventions are embedded in integrated care systems.

## Evidence Limits

- REACH VA was not randomized for the cost comparison.
- VA caregiver cost data were unavailable.
- The VA cost finding should not be treated as proof that all caregiver interventions reduce costs.
- This is not evidence that REACH improves dementia symptoms, slows dementia progression, or functions as clinical treatment.
