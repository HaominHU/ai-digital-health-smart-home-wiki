# Richter 2026 LLM Physician-Patient Communication Review

## Source Role

This PRISMA systematic review and meta-analysis synthesizes an early evidence base comparing written LLM-generated and physician-generated responses. It supports a narrow claim: in selected rating studies, AI responses were often judged more empathic. It does not establish authentic empathy, trustworthy advice, clinical safety, improved relationships, or better outcomes.

## Methods and Scope

- PubMed, Embase, Scopus, and Web of Science were searched and updated through March 31, 2025.
- Of 312 screened records and 58 full texts, 10 studies published from 2023 to 2025 were included.
- Studies were quantitative, predominantly cross-sectional, and focused on written communication; none was randomized or longitudinal.
- Eight studies examined empathy, and four contributed to a random-effects meta-analysis.
- The review protocol was not registered.

## Findings

- The four-study pooled standardized mean difference for perceived empathy favored LLM responses: 1.02 (95% CI 0.44-1.60; p<.001), with substantial heterogeneity (I2=73%).
- A separate two-study pooled odds ratio was 5.5 (95% CI 2.8-10.9) for rating AI responses as more empathic.
- AI responses were often longer; one included comparison reported 211 versus 52 words. Length may contribute to ratings and is not equivalent to better communication.
- Satisfaction findings were mixed. Trust was not directly measured in a consistent way; despite an abstract-level reference to trust studies, the body indicates that direct trust measurement was absent or insufficient.
- Some responses containing subtle harmful errors were still rated useful, illustrating how empathic tone can mask incorrect or unsafe content.

## Wiki Interpretation

LLMs may help draft clearer, more supportive written communication, but tone must be evaluated separately from factual accuracy, action safety, completeness, and role boundaries. High-stakes health content requires clinician review, source grounding, uncertainty display, and escalation paths. Perceived empathy is a communication-process outcome, not proof of authenticity or clinical benefit.

## Evidence Limits

- Only 10 early studies were included; four contributed to the main pooled analysis.
- No study evaluated real longitudinal physician-patient relationships, and spoken or multimodal communication was not represented.
- Risk of bias was assessed with more than one tool, and the meta-analysis involved imputation and heterogeneous designs.
- Model versions and prompting varied in a rapidly changing technical landscape.
- The evidence does not support autonomous clinical communication or unreviewed decision support.
