# Chen 2026 Evidence on Generative AI for Meaning-Centered Care in Later Life

## Source Role

This perspective article proposes a conceptual framework for using generative AI as bounded, human-supervised interactional infrastructure for meaning-centered care in later life. It is useful for AI care-interaction design, dignity and life-review framing, evaluation logic, and governance constraints.

It should not be used as empirical evidence that GenAI improves mental health, reduces loneliness, improves caregiver outcomes, or safely replaces human care.

## Objective

The article aims to reframe GenAI in late-life mental health away from symptom-centric screening, triage, classification, or autonomous chatbot support. It argues that older-adult mental health often involves existential and relational concerns, including loss of role, loneliness, dignity, continuity of self, and legacy.

## Method or Argument Base

This is a conceptual perspective article. The authors draw on meaning-centered psychotherapy, dignity therapy, life review, gerotranscendence theory, care ethics, and implementation science to propose a design and evaluation framework.

## Principal Contribution

The main contribution is a four-layer Sensing-Narrating-Connecting-Governing framework:

- Sensing: user-consented detection of language, voice, affective, behavioral, and contextual cues related to existential distress, loneliness, disrupted self-continuity, or meaning-related themes.
- Narrating: support for life review, narrative organization, values, roles, regrets, achievements, relationships, and legacy messages.
- Connecting: linking AI-supported narrative work to clinicians, nurses, social workers, family caregivers, or community workers when appropriate and under user control.
- Governing: boundaries, referral, evaluation, privacy protection, hallucination control, crisis protocols, cultural adaptation, auditability, and accountability.

## Source-Backed Points

- GenAI should not be framed as an autonomous substitute for clinicians, nurses, social workers, or family caregivers.
- Meaning-centered care cannot be reduced to an empathic chatbot because it depends on recognition, witnessing, emotional pacing, provenance of personal words, and accountable human relationships.
- AI-generated summaries or legacy artifacts should preserve the user's voice, avoid false memories, distinguish user-authored material from AI-generated text, and support control over what is shared.
- Human-in-the-loop review, escalation pathways, user control over sharing, and culturally responsive adaptation are central safeguards.
- Evaluation should go beyond technical metrics such as accuracy, response relevance, engagement, or completion rate.
- Relevant evaluation domains include existential outcomes, relational outcomes, safety outcomes, implementation outcomes, equity, workflow burden, cultural acceptability, and whether AI-flagged concerns are reviewed by humans.
- For older adults, risks may be amplified by bereavement, cognitive impairment, sensory limitations, social isolation, deference to perceived authority, digital literacy barriers, and family attitudes.

## Wiki Interpretation

For this wiki, the article is best used as design rationale for assistive, human-supervised GenAI in later-life care. It supports a shift from "AI therapist" metaphors toward accountable infrastructure that helps people, families, and care teams preserve meaning, dignity, and relational continuity.

The source also supports evaluating AI care systems by narrative fidelity, adverse-event monitoring, consent comprehension, accessibility, staff workload, escalation, and equity rather than engagement alone.

## Evidence Limits

- This is not a systematic review, trial, usability study, or implementation study.
- The framework is conceptual and not evidence that any current GenAI system meets these requirements.
- The article does not establish safety, effectiveness, feasibility, or adoption for older adults, caregivers, dementia care, palliative care, or community mental health settings.
- It should not be cited as clinical decision-making evidence.

## What This Source Does Not Answer

- Whether GenAI-supported life review or meaning-centered support improves outcomes.
- Which GenAI interface works best for older adults with sensory, cognitive, literacy, or language differences.
- How family caregivers should access, edit, or receive AI-generated legacy or care summaries.
- Which governance model is sufficient for clinical deployment.
