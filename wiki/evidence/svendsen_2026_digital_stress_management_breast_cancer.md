# Effects of digital stress-management interventions in breast cancer patients 18 months after intervention access

## Source Role

Provides adjacent patient-only evidence on long-term app-delivered stress management: neither intervention showed a statistically significant overall advantage over usual care at 18 months. This is not caregiver or gynecological-cancer evidence and not an equivalence result.

## Study Picture

- The Norwegian CABC trial randomized 430 women aged 21-69 to a cognitive behavioral intervention (CBI; n=140), mindfulness-based intervention (MBI; n=143), or usual-care control (n=147). Eligibility concerned selected breast-cancer subtypes and completion of a preceding digital registry survey; average enrollment was about eight months after diagnosis.
- Both interventions used StressProffen with ten educational/exercise modules, staged release, an introductory phone call, and brief follow-up calls at approximately weeks 1, 3, and 6. These were app-based psychosocial programs with human onboarding/support, not AI or smart-home interventions.
- At 18 months, 327/430 (76%) responded: CBI 106, MBI 103, control 118. Primary outcome: perceived stress (PSS-10). Secondary outcomes included health-related quality of life, mindfulness, fatigue, coping, anxiety/depression, and sleep. The authors describe intention-to-treat mixed models but exclude two people with no outcome data at any time point.
- Neither intervention showed a statistically significant overall advantage over control for perceived stress or secondary outcomes at 18 months. This is not an equivalence result and does not prove digital psychosocial interventions never work.
- At least seven modules were completed by 47.1% of CBI and 41.3% of MBI participants; only 22.9% and 25.2%, respectively, completed all ten. Median active days during months 6-18 were zero in both groups. Exploratory app-use analysis did not show an association with stress change. Extended access and sustained active use are different exposures.

## Evidence Limits and Interpretation

Moderator analyses suggested differential patterns by age, baseline stress, coping, expectations, and family context. These remain exploratory, involve multiple comparisons, and do not rescue the null overall trial as a broadly positive effectiveness result. Higher baseline stress predicted larger reductions across groups, with regression to the mean a possible contributor. The study was not powered to compare CBI against MBI.

Selection through a digital survey, restricted cancer subtypes, missing systemic-treatment information, differential nonresponse, and unmeasured control-group stress-management activity limit interpretation. The main article refers to supplementary numerical effect estimates; those supplementary files have not been archived or independently checked. Do not fabricate a precise primary effect size or claim equivalence from the plotted means.

**Adjacent-population interpretation:** predefine endpoints, distinguish access/use/outcomes, preserve null results, and validate subgroup hypotheses. Caregiver targeting or tailored caregiver support is a separate research hypothesis requiring direct studies.
