# Trust in digital health among older adults in remote areas of Italy: a latent class analysis

## Source Role

Separates favorable attitudes and willingness from low reported digital-health use among older adults in remote Italian areas. Trust is inferred from adoption-related proxies, rather than measured with a validated multidimensional trust instrument.

## Study Picture

- A 2022 paper survey targeted community-dwelling adults aged 65 and older in remote inner areas of Tuscany, Lombardy, Veneto, and Calabria. Of 2,188 respondents, 115 were excluded for missing one or more benchmark items, leaving 2,073.
- A general digital-health-gap survey supplied three proxy indicators: perceived usefulness for health monitoring, current use of health technologies, and willingness to try new tools. Large-print paper administration helped include people with digital-access difficulties. The questionnaire was not designed as a validated multidimensional trust scale.
- Latent class analysis identified a two-class solution interpreted as higher versus lower trust (reported approximately 61% and 38%; rounding retained). Use was reported by 17.12%, while 69.61% were willing to try new tools and 58.03% perceived health-monitoring usefulness. The evidence supports separating reported current use from favorable attitudes in this sample.
- Four separate univariate latent-class regression models considered age, education, sex, and chronic conditions. These are not jointly adjusted causal models. The paper reports no statistically significant covariate effects at its stated threshold; the education p-value is rounded to 0.05, so do not infer a clean null or a robust subgroup effect from that rounded value.

## Evidence Limits and Interpretation

Trust is inferred from proxies that also describe adoption readiness, not directly measured as a validated clinical or technical trust construct. Pooling users and nonusers obscures differences between pre-adoption expectations and experience-based trust; most respondents were nonusers. The cross-sectional design cannot establish that trust causes adoption, that use increases trust, or that training improves either. Current use was the weakest differentiating item.

Retain the distinction between response probabilities conditional on class membership and class-assignment probabilities; do not repeat the paper's loose probability wording as a causal risk ratio. Scope is remote-area Italian older adults, not all older adults, dementia, family caregivers, AI trust, or smart-home effectiveness.
