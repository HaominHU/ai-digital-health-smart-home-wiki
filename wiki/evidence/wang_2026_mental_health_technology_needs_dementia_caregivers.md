# A Taxonomy of Mental Health and Technology Needs for Alzheimer's and Dementia Caregivers

## Source Role

Maps distinct dementia caregiver mental-health needs to technology classes. This conceptual synthesis informs need coverage and outcome selection; it does not validate a triage instrument or establish comparative technology effects.

## Study Picture

- PubMed, APA PsycInfo, and Google Scholar were searched for English-language literature from January 1, 1975 through June 10, 2025. Population criteria allowed informal and formal AD/ADRD caregivers; synthesis emphasizes informal caregivers. Included work had to substantively discuss caregiver mental-health needs or psychological outcomes.
- The 52 included publications, dated 1997-2025, comprise 20 quantitative, 10 qualitative, 6 mixed-methods, and 16 review papers. This is a heterogeneous corpus containing secondary evidence; do not treat it as 52 independent intervention trials.
- Best-Fit Framework Synthesis uses Pearlin's caregiver stress-process model as a scaffold. The paper also integrates the team's earlier qualitative studies: 25 caregivers discussing mental-health needs and 16 caregivers engaging with Carey, a GPT-4o chatbot probe. The reported total is 41, mainly recruited through US online social-media communities. These are not new outcome data proving the taxonomy or chatbot effective.

## Main Contribution

The proposed taxonomy separates eight caregiver need domains: responding to disruptive care-recipient behaviors, navigating support systems, building self-efficacy, managing relationships, compassion fatigue, self-care, burnout, and emotional balance. Five technology groupings are mapped against these needs: monitoring tools, informational resources, social/emotional support technologies, task-management tools, and AI chatbots/hybrid platforms.

The reusable insight is that task coverage and a global burden score can conceal different underlying needs. The synthesis identifies relatively thin coverage of relationship strain, compassion fatigue, anticipatory grief, and sustained emotional adaptation. Monitoring-to-coaching links, stage-sensitive content, family coordination, and human escalation are design directions to test.

## Evidence Limits and Interpretation

- The mapping is conceptual and interpretive. It does not validate a screening instrument, predict an individual's needs, establish causal pathways, or estimate pooled comparative technology effects. No formal risk-of-bias weighting is reported in the main methods.
- Online, English-speaking, help-seeking participants limit reach to digitally excluded and linguistically diverse caregivers. Subgroup and longitudinal claims remain limited.
- Broad technology-benefit statements and the authors' proposed longitudinal AI roles must not become claims that LLMs prevent burnout or safely manage crises. Underlying cited trials require their own source review for stronger claims.
- PDF locators: methods pp. 3-6; need domains pp. 7-16; technology mapping pp. 17-26; discussion/limits pp. 27-31.
