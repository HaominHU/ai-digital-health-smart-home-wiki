# ChatGPT Health and ChatGPT for SCI Pre-Clinic Preparation Without Connected Records

## Question

What does this AMIA abstract suggest about source-reported ChatGPT Health versus standard ChatGPT for spinal cord injury pre-clinic preparation without connected records?

## Short Answer

The source suggests that a health-focused AI interface may provide more structured and actionable pre-clinic preparation support even without connected records, while prompt style may strongly affect empathetic tone. The evaluation is exploratory, small, and should not be treated as clinical effectiveness evidence.

## Evidence Summary

The abstract reports a two-stage comparative evaluation. First, source-reported ChatGPT Health and standard ChatGPT were compared on two SCI benchmark questions. Second, two SCI vignettes were evaluated using concise clinical prompts and natural care-recipient-style prompts. Eight conversations were scored by an AI judge assigned a PM&R Physician Assistant role on clinical accuracy, usability/acceptability/feasibility, empathetic tone, and actionability of advice.

Both systems produced nearly identical responses to general SCI knowledge questions in the comparability screen. In scenario-based evaluation, the source-reported health-focused interface led on usability/acceptability/feasibility and actionability, while standard ChatGPT scored slightly higher on clinical accuracy and empathetic tone. Care-recipient-style prompts outperformed concise clinical prompts overall, mainly through higher empathetic tone.

## What Is Well Supported

- SCI pre-clinic preparation is framed as an underserved need involving complex health concerns and self-advocacy.
- The source highlights privacy and trust concerns around linking EHR or wearable data to commercial AI platforms.
- Prompt style affected empathetic tone in this exploratory setup.
- Structured outputs such as portal message drafts and multi-deliverable preparation materials are relevant design directions.

## What Is Uncertain

- Product/model behavior and product names are source-reported and may change.
- AI-judge scores are not clinical outcomes.
- Record-connected AI use was not evaluated.
- Context contamination was observed as an accuracy issue.

## Gaps

- Evaluation with actual SCI care recipients, caregivers, and clinicians.
- Safety and privacy evaluation for connected-record use.
- Larger scenario sets and human expert review.
- Evidence about whether AI preparation improves visit quality, self-advocacy, or outcomes.

## Design or Research Implications

- Design AI pre-clinic preparation tools as communication and organization support, not autonomous clinical advice.
- Consider care-recipient-style prompting as part of human-centered AI design.
- Treat EHR and wearable linkage as a separate consent, trust, privacy, and security layer.

## Privacy and Security Notes

AI pre-clinic preparation should minimize sensitive data exposure, avoid requiring commercial-platform record linkage, preserve user control, and clearly separate preparation support from clinical decision-making.
