# Aneshensel and Avison 2015 Evidence on Pearlin's Stress Process

## Source Role

This article is a readable modern overview of Leonard I. Pearlin's stress-process legacy. It should be used as a secondary conceptual gateway into the Pearlin stress-process architecture, not as a substitute for the original 1981 stress-process article or the 1990 caregiving-specific stress-process article.

## Source-Backed Points

- Pearlin's stress-process work shifted stress research from isolated acute events toward chronic strains embedded in everyday roles and social structure.
- The stress-process model emphasizes relationships among stressors, personal and social resources, and mental health outcomes rather than isolated one-stressor or one-resource explanations.
- Stressors are socially patterned: social status, inequality, roles, relationships, and institutions shape exposure to stressors and access to protective resources.
- Stress proliferation matters because stressors can arise from other stressors, accumulate across domains, and deplete the resources that people need to cope.
- Personal and social resources, including mastery, self-concept, coping, and social support, can mediate or moderate stress effects.
- Pearlin's later work extended the stress-process model to life-course and caregiving contexts, including AIDS caregiving and Alzheimer's disease caregiving.

## Wiki Interpretation

For this wiki, the source supports a caregiver burden ontology that separates:

- Primary stressors.
- Secondary role and intrapsychic strains.
- Stressor meaning or appraisal.
- Coping, mastery, self-concept, and social support.
- Psychological, physical, behavioral, social, and economic outcomes.
- Social-structural context, including inequity, role constraints, and resource access.

This model is especially useful for designing caregiver-support systems that avoid treating caregiver distress as only an individual deficit.

## Evidence Limits

- The local PDF is not the original Pearlin et al. 1981 "The Stress Process" article.
- The local PDF is not Pearlin et al. 1990 "Caregiving and the Stress Process: An Overview of Concepts and Their Measures."
- The source supports conceptual framing, not caregiver intervention effectiveness, AI effectiveness, smart-home effectiveness, prevalence, or condition-specific outcomes.
- Any claims about Pearlin's specific 1990 caregiving measures should wait for that article to be ingested or separately added as an external citation candidate.
