# Person-environment fit in aging in place: A digital support layer and inequalities in environmental satisfaction among urban empty-nest older adults in China

## Source Role

Studies perceived fit across home, community, social, and digital environments. It measures older-adult satisfaction and conditional associations, not technology effectiveness or caregiver outcomes.

## Study or Framework Picture

- An exploratory sequential mixed-methods design used constructivist grounded theory interviews with 30 older adults from 15 highly aged communities in Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, Chongqing, and Tianjin.
- Stratified purposive and maximum-variation sampling broadened community and individual contexts. The paper concerns community-dwelling adults aged 60 or older who do not live with adult children; empty-nest status should not be equated with living alone.
- The qualitative analysis yielded 95 initial concepts, nine axial categories, and four core dimensions. These informed a 125-item satisfaction instrument covering physical home (35 items), community public environment (60), social support/psychological well-being (8), and age-friendly smart technology (22).
- Of 1,888 questionnaires distributed, 1,613 were returned and 1,510 retained after screening. Internal development and validation samples each contained 755 participants. Multivariable regressions and two theory-driven interaction sets examined conditional associations.
- Qualitative and quantitative evidence were integrated through construct development, theory-informed linking, and joint interpretation.

## Main Findings and Contribution

- The four-factor model showed reported fit indices CFI 0.992, TLI 0.992, SRMR 0.022, and RMSEA 0.006. The nine-subdimension model provided additional within-sample structure; external measurement equivalence remains untested.
- Satisfaction was highest for social support/psychological well-being and lowest for the community public environment (reported mean 3.532).
- Marital status, residential distance from adult children, income, functional ability, age, community age, and management model were associated with satisfaction differences. Mild functional limitation was associated with lower community-environment satisfaction than full independence (reported standardized beta -0.127; Cohen's f-squared 0.02).
- The gap in community-environment satisfaction associated with mild limitation was more pronounced in older communities. Higher social support was associated with a narrower smart-technology satisfaction gap for adults aged 90 or older.
- The digital layer is modeled as both a resource (monitoring, reminders, interaction, remote care) and a source of demands (operation, comprehension, trust, maintenance). It remains embedded in physical and social environments.

## Evidence Limits and Conceptual Boundaries

- Cross-sectional associations cannot establish temporal relationships, causal buffering by support, or that smart technology improves aging-in-place outcomes. Community exposure and participation are hypothesized explanations rather than measured mechanisms.
- Satisfaction is not actual technology use, activity frequency, service receipt, independence, or clinical benefit. Agency and belonging are interpretive constructs requiring direct longitudinal measurement.
- The sample is most directly applicable to highly aged communities in five high-density Chinese cities. The coefficients and satisfaction ordering need external replication across cities, rural settings, and cultures.
- The 125-item scale has substantial response burden (reported median completion time 41.7 minutes). Split-sample validation supports internal reproducibility; independent validation, measurement-invariance testing, and a shorter instrument are future work.
- The study does not measure caregiver burden or intervention effects. Functional limitation should not be equated with normal aging, disability of every cause, or a specific chronic disease.

## Wiki Interpretation and Design Implications

**Cross-source interpretation:** digitally supported aging in place requires fit among individual capacity, housing/community conditions, available assistance, and technology demands. Subjective appraisal and observed task performance should be measured separately.

**Design direction to test:** combine setup, task practice, and continued assistance while measuring helper workload, user autonomy, actual use, and environmental barriers. Family help should be consented and resourced; this paper does not establish that transferring support work to relatives improves outcomes.

## Privacy and Safety

Use published aggregate results and generalized themes. Do not reproduce participant quotations or author contact information. Health and household data require consent, minimum necessary access, secure storage, retention limits, auditability, and attention to re-identification and unequal access. Proposed clinical alerts require validation, human oversight, and defined escalation responsibilities. This source is for research, design, and knowledge support.
