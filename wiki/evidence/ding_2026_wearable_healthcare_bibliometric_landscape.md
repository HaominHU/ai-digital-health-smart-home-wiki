# Ding 2026 Wearable Healthcare Bibliometric Landscape

## Source Role

This large dual-database bibliometric review maps how wearable-healthcare research developed from 2016 through April 2026. Its core contribution is field structure: the literature is moving from isolated sensor and hardware work toward connected, patient-centered ecosystems involving telemedicine, the Internet of Medical Things, AI-supported analysis, EHR integration, personalized monitoring, rehabilitation, cost, and quality of life.

Use the paper to identify research directions and concentrations. Do not use bibliometric prominence as evidence that a wearable system is clinically effective, accurate, safe, or cost-effective.

## Methods and Scale

- Web of Science Core Collection and Scopus were searched for January 1, 2016, through April 30, 2026; records were retrieved May 24, 2026.
- The search was limited to English-language articles and broadly combined wearable devices or sensors with healthcare or medical care.
- Initial retrieval included 16,284 Web of Science and 29,355 Scopus records. Date, article-type, and language screening retained 9,096 and 11,622 records, respectively.
- DOI-first, fuzzy title/year/author matching, and contextual review produced 12,812 unique articles.
- CiteSpace 6.4.R1 and VOSviewer supported collaboration, co-citation, co-occurrence, clustering, and keyword-burst analyses using two-year slices and the top 50 items per slice.
- Search exports, scripts, deduplication logs, term-cleaning resources, and the final dataset were deposited in Zenodo.

## Field-Scale Findings

Annual publication output increased from fewer than 500 articles in 2016-2017 to 647 in 2018, 992 in 2020, 1,192 in 2021, and 2,498 in 2025. The paper reports 1,076 records for 2026 despite an April 30 cutoff; that number should not be described as a complete or half-year total.

China led publication count with 3,446 records. The top-country table reports 2,765 for the United States, while the narrative reports 2,756; the table value is retained as the more structured result. The Chinese Academy of Sciences led the institutional table with 344 records and 20,302 citations.

## Wiki Interpretation

The review supports wearables as a distinct technology lens spanning consumer tracking, clinical-grade monitoring, smart textiles, biosensors, rehabilitation devices, and connected remote-monitoring ecosystems. It also supports studying interoperability, clinician and caregiver workflow integration, training, privacy, accessibility, and meaningful outcomes alongside sensing performance.

Bibliometric clusters are not condition evidence. Breast-milk, cancer, chronic-wound, and other clusters require direct source ingest before they can support condition-specific claims.

## Evidence Limits

- Bibliometric attention and citation structure do not establish study quality, diagnostic validity, clinical utility, safety, effectiveness, or cost-effectiveness.
- English-language, database, publication, and citation-window biases may underrepresent localized, non-English, recent, or practice-oriented work.
- Co-authorship does not establish intellectual contribution, and citation counts can reflect field behavior, retractions, or manipulation.
- The clinical-application discussion is not a systematic quality appraisal of the cited studies.
- Internal inconsistencies in the 2026 period, US publication count, and health-monitoring burst strength require conservative reuse.
