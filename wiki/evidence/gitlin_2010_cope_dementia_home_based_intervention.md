# Gitlin 2010 Evidence on COPE Dementia Home-Based Intervention

## Source Role

This randomized trial is useful as dementia-specific dyadic intervention evidence for home-based caregiver training, environmental tailoring, medical review, and short-term caregiver and care-recipient outcomes.

## Full-Study Picture

- Problem/gap: Dementia-related functional decline burdens family caregivers, and few large trials had tested nonpharmacologic approaches to support patient function and caregiver well-being.
- Population/condition/setting: 237 randomized community-living dyads in Pennsylvania; patients had dementia and family caregivers provided substantial oversight or care.
- Intervention/system: COPE delivered up to 12 home or telephone contacts over 4 months. Occupational therapists and an advanced practice nurse assessed patient capabilities, home environment, medication/health issues, caregiver communication, and caregiver-identified concerns.
- Key innovation: Biobehavioral environmental tailoring realigned home and task demands with dementia-related capabilities while training caregivers in problem solving, communication, activity engagement, home safety, task simplification, and stress reduction.
- Comparator: Control caregivers received three telephone calls and educational materials.
- Main measures: Patient functional dependence, IADL/ADL dependence, quality of life, activity engagement, agitated behaviors; caregiver perceived change in well-being and confidence using activities.
- Key results at 4 months: COPE improved overall functional dependence, IADL dependence, activity engagement, caregiver well-being, and caregiver confidence using activities. It did not significantly improve caregiver-rated patient quality of life or agitated behavior frequency.
- Durability: No statistically significant standardized outcome differences were found at 9 months, although COPE caregivers reported greater perceived benefits.

## Wiki Interpretation

Use COPE as evidence that home-based dementia caregiver training plus environmental and medical review can improve short-term dyadic outcomes. It is especially useful for home environment design, capability-aligned activity support, caregiver assessment, and core outcome selection.

## Evidence Limits

- The trial cannot isolate active treatment components.
- Many outcomes rely on caregiver proxy report.
- Volunteers may have been more motivated than nonparticipants.
- Male caregiver attrition was higher.
- Long-term standardized effects were not sustained at 9 months.
- This is not AI, mHealth, smart-home, or dementia disease-modification evidence.
