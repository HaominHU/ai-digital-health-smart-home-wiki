# Dissertation Evidence on Multi-Component mHealth Support for Family Caregivers

## Question

What does Hu's dissertation contribute to the wiki's understanding of mHealth support for family caregivers of people with chronic conditions and disabilities?

## Short Answer

The dissertation provides source-backed design and early evaluation evidence for an adaptive, multi-component caregiver mHealth app. It supports reusable caregiver hubs around self-care, training, information access, care coordination, reminders, emotional/social support, and adaptive modular design, but it does not establish clinical effectiveness.

## Evidence Summary

The dissertation developed and evaluated a family caregiver app through user-centered approaches: a focus group, co-design workshops, and a pilot test. The app targeted family caregivers of people with chronic conditions and disabilities, with wiki-relevant condition contexts including spinal cord injury and gynecological cancer.

Design concepts included general caregiver modules, condition-specific content delivered through reusable interfaces, condition-specific modules, risk assessment, goals/plans, self-care tools, educational modules, and a resource library.

Pilot testing included 12 family caregivers and reported no loss to follow-up. The average MAUQ score was 5.49, with positive ease-of-use and interface/satisfaction scores, while usefulness was comparatively lower. Usage logs showed high engagement with My Tools and educational modules and lower use of Resources Library and Set Goals/Make Plans.

## What Is Well Supported

- Family caregivers need direct self-care and training support, not only tools for monitoring care recipients.
- Caregiver mHealth should support both general caregiver needs and condition-specific content.
- Reminders, clearer prompts, larger fonts, module integration, and adaptive content matter for caregiver engagement.
- Caregiver support should address stress, burnout, social isolation, reliable information, care coordination, and public resource navigation.
- Android/iOS differences, internet dependence, and cross-platform reliability can affect usability.

## What Is Uncertain

- Clinical effectiveness and health outcome effects are not established.
- Condition-specific comparative effects are not established.
- AI/GenAI, RAG, wearables, passive monitoring, and clinician-portal support are future directions rather than implemented findings in this dissertation.

## Gaps

- Larger trials across broader caregiver populations.
- Dyadic caregiver-care recipient studies.
- Dementia caregiver testing, noted as future work but not performed in the dissertation.
- Privacy and security validation for shared access, monitoring, AI, wearables, and clinical workflow integration.

## Design or Research Implications

- Build caregiver mHealth as an adaptive modular system.
- Treat family caregivers as direct intervention recipients.
- Separate reusable caregiver challenges from condition overlays.
- Keep non-priority conditions from participant composition as source context unless the wiki priority list changes.

## Privacy and Security Notes

Caregiver mHealth systems should include consent, access control, role boundaries, secure storage, auditability, privacy-preserving shared access, and care recipient autonomy safeguards.
