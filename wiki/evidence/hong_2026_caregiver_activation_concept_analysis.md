# Hong 2026 Caregiver Activation Concept Analysis

## Source Role

This Walker and Avant concept analysis gives the wiki a reusable caregiver activation model. Caregiver activation is a multidimensional, dynamic state of readiness that makes a caregiver willing and able to learn, adapt, collaborate, and act. It is broader than task performance and may begin before formal caregiving starts.

## Methods

- Eleven English- and Chinese-language databases were searched from inception through October 2025, with dictionary and reference-list supplementation.
- Searches retrieved 19,572 records; 51 studies were included.
- Two reviewers independently screened and extracted data, with a third person resolving disagreement.
- The concept analysis was not registered.

## Defining Attributes

1. **Care capabilities:** integrated care-management and caregiver self-care competence, including disease management, monitoring, emergency response, lifestyle support, clinician communication, coordination, emotional regulation, stress management, health maintenance, and support seeking.
2. **Care autonomy:** ability and motivation to initiate, organize, and carry out care while recognizing when professional assistance or collaboration is needed. Autonomy is not isolation from professional help.
3. **Care resilience:** sustained psychological adaptation and care capacity under long-term or recurring stress.
4. **Self-efficacy in care provision:** confidence in care tasks, clinician communication, collaboration, and role-specific decisions.

Communication connects capabilities, autonomy, and self-efficacy rather than forming a separate fifth dimension.

## Antecedents and Consequences

Four antecedents were identified: care-recipient dependency; the caregiver's intermediary and collaborative role with clinicians; insufficient caregiving proficiency, including fragmented information and digital or AI skill demands; and physical, emotional, social, occupational, or financial burden.

The paper groups reported consequences into improved health-service access or use, caregiver quality of life and well-being, and enhanced care management, help seeking, advocacy, and responsiveness. These are associations drawn from underlying literature, not causal effects established by the concept analysis.

## Concept Boundaries

- Activation emphasizes readiness and capability: being willing and able to act. Empowerment emphasizes influence, agency, advocacy, shared decisions, and redistribution of power; activation may precede empowerment.
- Self-efficacy is one component of activation, not the entire construct.
- Health literacy is narrower than activation's behavioral, motivational, relational, and adaptive dimensions.
- Parental activation is a context-specific subtype with child-development and educational responsibilities.
- High activation or autonomy must not shift clinical responsibility onto families or encourage unsupported decisions.

## Empirical Referents

The paper identifies the Parent-Patient Activation Measure, Parent-Patient Activation Measure for Mental Health, Parent Activation Measure for Developmental Disabilities, Caregiver-Patient Activation Measure, and Managing Your Loved One's Health. Cross-cultural and factor-structure limits mean no measure should be treated as universally validated.

## Caregiver Knowledge-Network Adaptation

The wiki should treat activation as a dynamic assessment and tailoring construct linking caregiver burden, capabilities, self-care, autonomy, resilience, self-efficacy, communication, collaboration, service navigation, and support seeking. Low activation is a signal to strengthen system support, training, access, and role clarity - not a caregiver deficit label. Intervention evaluation should test whether activation changes, for whom, under what conditions, and whether change improves caregiver or care-process outcomes without transferring professional responsibility.

## Evidence Limits

- Language restriction and variation across the 51 studies may introduce selection and interpretation bias.
- Much of the underlying evidence comes from specific pediatric, oncology, dementia, autism, mental-health, or sociocultural contexts.
- Evidence for broad care-recipient outcomes is limited, and service-use evidence is concentrated in pediatric mental health.
- The paper clarifies a construct; it does not validate a new measure or establish that increasing activation causes better outcomes.
