# Evaluation of Personas and Daily Activity Maps to Inform Clinical Care and Socially Assistive Robot Training and Design for Persons With Dementia: Mixed Methods Study

## Source Role

Evaluates stakeholders’ perceptions of fictional composite personas and daily activity maps for dementia technology design. Ratings inform formative design and requested contextual detail; they do not establish robot performance or care outcomes.

## Study Picture

- An expert design team created nine fictional composite personas and corresponding daily activity maps from clinical/caregiving experience and representative schemas. These are not nine enrolled patients or patient-level longitudinal records.
- Fifteen of 19 invited stakeholders participated in purposively recruited Zoom interviews in March-April 2025. Only two were family caregivers; the remainder represented clinical, facility-care, research, and robot-design roles. Persons with dementia did not participate.
- Stakeholders chose familiar/relatable persona-DAM pairs, contributing 36 evaluations, averaging 2.4 pairs per person. The same participant's evaluations are not independent observations.
- An adapted 12-item Persona Perception Scale covered credibility, completeness, clarity, willingness to use, and empathy. The original scale was developed outside dementia care; the adapted scale and author-created overall composite were not psychometrically validated here.
- Evaluation-level overall medians were 4.79/5 for personas and 4.76/5 for DAMs. Participant-level descriptive analyses showed similar patterns. Ceiling effects were prominent; completeness was relatively lower. Three qualitative themes concerned richer personal context, clinical/functional context, and perceived translation into useful design tools.

## Evidence Limits and Interpretation

This is evidence of favorable stakeholder perceptions and requested design refinements. It does not demonstrate reduced agitation, falls, caregiver burden, improved robot performance, accurate clinical reasoning, or actual long-term adoption. Small role subgroups, professional-network recruitment, self-selected materials, repeated ratings, and no direct dementia-participant input constrain conclusions.

Proposed design implications: distinguish biographical preferences, cultural/language context, daily routines, functional capabilities, and comorbidity/treatment context; avoid attributing every behavior to dementia. Treat contextual data as a potential sensitive data burden requiring minimization, controlled sharing, consent/assent processes, and human oversight. Do not collect actual individual care profiles into this wiki or treat stakeholder interest as authorization for clinical automation.
