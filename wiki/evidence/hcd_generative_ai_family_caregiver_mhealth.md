# Human-Centered Design Recommendations for Generative AI in Family Caregiver mHealth

## Question

What caregiver-informed design directions does this AMIA abstract suggest for future generative AI in family caregiver mHealth?

## Short Answer

The source suggests that generative AI may be most useful as an adaptive layer for proactive engagement, personalized education, and emotional/social support in caregiver mHealth. It does not report implemented or evaluated generative AI features.

## Evidence Summary

The abstract reports iterative human-centered design work around the iMHere Family Caregiver App. It included a literature review and focus group, co-design workshops with family caregivers of individuals with neurological conditions, and a two-arm cohort evaluation with family caregivers of individuals with neurological conditions and gynecological cancer.

Reported themes included app interaction model, content design, and emerging needs. These led to recommendations for intuitive navigation, just-in-time prompts, proactive app-driven engagement, plain-language condition-specific education, trajectory-sensitive adaptive content, and support for emotional and social needs.

## What Is Well Supported

- Family caregiver mHealth design should account for caregiver time restrictions.
- Plain-language and trajectory-sensitive content are important for caregiver-facing educational resources.
- Emotional check-ins, peer connection, and situation-specific contacts were recurring caregiver-envisioned needs.
- Future AI design directions can be organized into agentic interaction, adaptive content, and emotional/social support layers.

## What Is Uncertain

- Whether generative AI improves caregiver outcomes, engagement, usability, or emotional support was not tested in this abstract.
- Subgroup-specific findings for gynecological cancer or neurological conditions are not detailed.
- SCI relevance is a wiki mapping from broad neurological-condition caregiver evidence, not a direct SCI-specific source claim.

## Gaps

- Implementation evidence for generative AI features.
- Safety and privacy validation for AI-mediated emotional support, peer matching, and dynamic resource directories.
- Larger and more condition-specific evaluations.

## Design or Research Implications

- Treat generative AI as a bounded support layer inside caregiver mHealth.
- Separate universal caregiver modules, reusable condition-specific interfaces, and condition-unique needs.
- Track health literacy, trajectory stage, and caregiver time burden as design variables.

## Privacy and Security Notes

Future AI-enabled caregiver support should address data minimization, consent, access control, auditability, bias, human oversight, escalation boundaries, and risk of over-reliance. Outputs should remain research, design, or knowledge support unless separately reviewed for another non-clinical use.
