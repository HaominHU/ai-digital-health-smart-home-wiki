# Malhotra 2025 CareBuddy Mobile Care Ecosystem for Dementia Caregiving

## Summary

Malhotra et al. 2025 reports the development and mixed-methods usability/acceptability testing of CareBuddy, a theory-driven mobile care ecosystem for caregivers of individuals with dementia.

## Source-Backed Findings

- CareBuddy was developed using user-centered design and guided by the stress process model.
- User requirements were informed by interviews with 22 participants, including family caregivers, community dementia providers, clinicians, and policymakers.
- The app includes personalized assessments, tailored dementia symptom management strategies, a RAG-supported AI chatbot, GPS location monitoring, care planning, provider messaging, QR code multi-caregiver coordination, telemedicine links, helpline access, financial/legal/future-planning content, self-care worksheets, calendar reminders, and moderated peer support.
- The chatbot uses GPT-4 as the default language model and GPT-3.5 for contextual analysis based on a knowledge database, according to the source.
- The chatbot uses a confidence-based retrieval approach over internal Q&A content and external sources and directs urgent or sensitive cases to immediate resources such as helplines.
- Reported privacy/security measures include de-identification, delinking sensitive records from user profiles, AWS cloud storage, restricted administrator access, multifactor authentication, privacy policy, End User License Agreement, and app-store review.
- Phase 1 included 18 participants across 3 iterative rounds; later rounds exceeded the SUS usability benchmark.
- Phase 2 included 10 caregivers using the app for 1 month and produced high MAUQ usability/satisfaction scores.
- Participants valued curated dementia content, symptom management support, self-care resources, service directory access, peer forum support, and time-saving access to resources.

## Wiki-Relevant Interpretation

This source is direct dementia caregiver mHealth evidence for a multicomponent app ecosystem. It supports design reasoning around modular caregiver support, dementia-specific content, AI/RAG chatbot safeguards, GPS monitoring, peer/professional support, provider integration, and privacy/security design.

## Evidence Limits

- Development, usability, acceptability, and short-term engagement evidence only.
- Small sample size and short follow-up.
- Does not establish caregiver outcome effectiveness, care-recipient safety, burden reduction, or sustained adoption.
- The planned hybrid type 1 randomized controlled trial is future work.
- Product/model details are source-reported and should be re-verified before current-state AI product claims.
