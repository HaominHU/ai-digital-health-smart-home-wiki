# Kolakowski 2026 Digital Health Intrinsic Capacity Feasibility

## Source Role

This two-month, three-country pilot evaluates feasibility, usability, and early acceptance of the CAREUP digital healthy-aging platform among older adults and a smaller caregiver group. It informs multi-device onboarding and digital-equity design; it does not demonstrate health improvement or validate the platform's AI.

## Study and Platform

- The study ran from September through December 2024 in Italy, Romania, and Austria.
- Sixty-six older adults and 17 caregivers enrolled; 61 participants completed the two-month assessment.
- The platform combined older-adult and caregiver applications, a clinician dashboard, questionnaires, games, locomotion exercises, and connected measures for steps, blood pressure, weight, oxygen saturation, glucose, grip strength, and sleep.
- The system used HTTPS, authentication, cloud storage, and pseudonymization.
- Although described as AI-driven, the pilot did not evaluate AI accuracy, safety, calibration, or decision quality.

## Findings

- Mean System Usability Scale score was 67.62 for older adults and 66.2 for caregivers.
- Older-adult country means were 45.94 in Austria, 67.33 in Italy, and 75.54 in Romania, demonstrating important implementation variation.
- Older-adult User Experience Questionnaire scores were 1.66 hedonic, 1.23 pragmatic, and 1.45 overall; caregiver overall experience was 0.99.
- Among 61 completers, 49 reported a positive experience, 48 would recommend the platform, 39 wanted to continue, 8 would use it occasionally, and 11 declined continued use.
- Barriers included connectivity, device overload, interface and graph interpretation, repetitive questionnaires, complexity, and digital-literacy or training needs.
- Secondary health measures were generally stable. The study was not powered or designed to establish improvement.

## Wiki Interpretation

Multi-component healthy-aging platforms should reduce device and questionnaire burden, provide role-specific onboarding and ongoing support, make graphs interpretable, and test implementation across settings rather than relying on aggregate usability. Intrinsic capacity is a healthy-aging framework and must remain distinct from chronic disease, disability, treatment effects, and caregiver capacity, even when these interact.

A foundational WHO source is still needed before the wiki makes stronger claims about the intrinsic-capacity framework itself.

## Evidence Limits

- Short follow-up, convenience sampling, and a technology-readiness level of 5-6 limit generalization and maturity claims.
- Exclusion of people with major visual or hearing impairments and serious conditions may understate accessibility needs.
- Small samples, especially by country and caregiver role, make comparisons exploratory.
- The study supports feasibility and usability, not effectiveness, sustained adoption, clinical benefit, or AI performance.
