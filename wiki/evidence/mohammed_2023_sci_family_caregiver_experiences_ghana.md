# Mohammed 2023 SCI Family Caregiver Experiences in Ghana

## Source Summary

Mohammed et al. 2023 is a qualitative descriptive phenomenology study of family caregivers of people with spinal cord injury at Komfo Anokye Teaching Hospital in Ghana. The study interviewed 10 family caregivers and identified four main themes: becoming a caregiver, roles of the caregiver, burden of caregiving, and coping strategies.

## Key Evidence

- Entry into SCI caregiving was often sudden, unplanned, and emotionally shocking.
- Caregivers reported uncertainty about SCI consequences, symptoms, unfamiliar hospital environments, and inpatient care demands.
- In this setting, family caregivers were actively involved in hands-on inpatient care such as bathing, feeding, grooming, diaper changing, transport, and turning.
- Caregiver roles included ADL support such as bathing, dressing, feeding, toileting, and bowel/bladder management.
- Caregiver roles also included IADL support such as transportation, cooking, errands, housekeeping, financial management, and care-recipient business or money management.
- Caregivers reported physical burden including body pain, sleeplessness, tiredness, weakness, and sickness.
- Caregivers reported financial burden because caregiving reduced work time while increasing care-related expenses.
- Coping strategies included acceptance, personal care, religious faith and prayer, hope, social support, family financial help, childcare help, business support, and encouragement.
- The authors recommend training, counselling, and social support services for family caregivers of people with SCI.

## Wiki-Relevant Interpretation

This source provides SCI-specific caregiver evidence and should not be diluted into only general caregiver burden. It is especially useful for grounding caregiver onboarding, training, assessment, and practical support needs in condition-specific lived experience.

## Design Implications

- SCI caregiver tools should include onboarding and training for ADL/IADL support and bowel/bladder-related care tasks where appropriate.
- Assessment should track caregiver physical capacity, sleep disruption, financial strain, preparedness, social support, coping strategies, and emotional shock.
- Resource navigation should include financial strain and practical family support, not only medical education.
- Emotional and social support should account for religious, family, and community coping patterns without assuming they substitute for formal support.

## Gaps and Cautions

- Small sample from one hospital in Ghana.
- Mostly female caregiver sample.
- The findings are qualitative and context-specific, not prevalence estimates.
- The study recommends supports but does not evaluate an intervention.
