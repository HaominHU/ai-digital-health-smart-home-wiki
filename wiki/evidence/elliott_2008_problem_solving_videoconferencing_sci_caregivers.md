# Elliott 2008 Videoconferencing Problem-Solving Training for SCI Caregivers

## Source Role

Elliott et al. 2008 is SCI-specific randomized trial evidence for individualized problem-solving training delivered to family caregivers by videoconferencing. Use it as caregiver intervention, telehealth delivery, dyadic outcome, and implementation-limitation evidence.

## Full-Study Picture

- Problem/gap: SCI family caregivers can experience substantial emotional and physical strain, and clinic-based support may be hard to access because of mobility, distance, and health constraints.
- Population/condition/setting: 61 family caregivers of people with SCI and their care recipients; recruitment came from inpatient rehabilitation and community sources.
- Care-recipient context: 25 care recipients had paraplegia and 36 had tetraplegia; 39 had incomplete lesions and 22 had complete lesions; time since SCI ranged from 1 month to 324 months.
- Intervention/system: Individualized problem-solving training delivered through monthly videoconferencing sessions for one year after an initial in-home assessment, orientation, and videophone setup.
- Comparator: Education-only control with scheduled contact, educational materials, videoconferencing contact, and problem scenarios, but without problem-solving principles, coping skills, or solution critique.
- Key innovation: A home-based telehealth model used videoconferencing to deliver longitudinal, problem-tailored caregiver coaching.
- Main measures: Caregiver social problem-solving, depression, life satisfaction, and selected SF-36 quality-of-life scales; care-recipient depression, life satisfaction, and selected SF-36 quality-of-life scales.
- Key results: Attrition was high, with 28 caregivers discontinuing and older caregivers more likely to remain. Intent-to-treat analyses projected a significant decrease in caregiver depression for PST, and efficacy analyses suggested this was pronounced at 6 months. Care recipients of caregivers receiving PST reported gains in social functioning over time. Significant effects were not found for measured constructive or dysfunctional caregiver problem-solving style scores.

## Wiki Interpretation

This source supports remote, problem-tailored SCI caregiver intervention design and shows why dyadic outcomes should be evaluated separately from caregiver outcomes. It should not be simplified into a broad claim that videoconferencing or PST improves all caregiver outcomes.

## Design Implications

- Start SCI caregiver support from personally salient problems rather than generic information alone.
- Use structured problem definition, optimism/orientation, alternative generation, decision-making, implementation, and solution review as coaching logic.
- Evaluate caregiver depression, problem-solving process measures, quality of life, retention, and care-recipient outcomes separately.
- Treat remote support as an access strategy that still needs attrition, fidelity, mechanism, and representativeness evaluation.

## Evidence Limits

- High attrition limits confidence and raises reach/retention concerns.
- The sample and technology-era delivery model constrain generalization.
- Intervention and control fidelity methods were limited.
- Mechanisms behind caregiver depression and care-recipient social-functioning effects are unclear.
- This is not AI-agent, smart-home, SCI disease-treatment, or autonomous clinical decision evidence.
