# Cheng 2020 Meta-Review of Dementia Caregiver Interventions

## Source Context

Cheng and Zhang 2020 is a BMC Geriatrics meta-review of systematic reviews and meta-analyses on nonpharmacological interventions for informal dementia caregivers.

Original citation:

- `2020_cheng_meta-review-dementia-caregiver-interventions`: Cheng S-T, Zhang F. A comprehensive meta-review of systematic reviews and meta-analyses on nonpharmacological interventions for informal dementia caregivers. BMC Geriatrics. 2020;20:137. DOI: `10.1186/s12877-020-01547-2`. Source file: `sources/papers/cg_system_core/13_cheng_2020_meta_review_dementia_caregivers.pdf`.

## Review Scope

- Search scope: MEDLINE, PsycINFO, CINAHL, and Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews.
- Publication window: 2006-2018.
- Included sources: 60 peer-reviewed systematic reviews and/or meta-analyses, covering more than 500 nonoverlapping intervention studies.
- Population: Informal caregivers of people with dementia receiving community- or home-based care.
- Exclusions: Pharmacological interventions, process evaluations, scoping reviews, institutionalized care-recipient contexts, and care-recipient-only interventions without caregiver involvement.

## Intervention and Outcome Taxonomy

Intervention categories included psychoeducation, counseling/psychotherapy, occupational therapy, mindfulness-based interventions, communication skills training, respite/day care, home care/support, physical activity, technology-based interventions, multicomponent interventions, dyadic interventions, and miscellaneous categories.

Common outcome domains included burden, depression, anxiety, quality of life or well-being, mastery/self-efficacy/competence, social support, communication knowledge/skills, and selected care-recipient-related outcomes.

## Review-Level Findings

- Depression was the most consistently modifiable caregiver outcome.
- Stronger review-level support appeared for psychoeducation, counseling/psychotherapy, mindfulness, and multicomponent interventions for depression.
- Psychoeducation had some support for quality of life.
- Psychoeducation, occupational therapy, and multicomponent interventions had emerging support for mastery or competence.
- Communication training supported communication knowledge and skills.
- Anxiety and social support had little clear evidence of intervention benefit.
- Burden evidence was mixed or weak; when effects were found, they were often small and hard to attribute to specific intervention types.
- Support groups and respite were generally ineffective in this review-level synthesis.
- The evidence did not support dyadic programs as superior to caregiver-only programs, nor did it show that multicomponent interventions reliably had broader effects than single-component programs.

## Review-Level Takeaway

Dementia caregiver intervention effects are domain-specific. Intervention selection should be tied to the target outcome and mechanism, rather than assuming that multicomponent, dyadic, group, individual, technology-based, or respite approaches are inherently better.

For this wiki, Cheng 2020 should serve as the current synthesis anchor for dementia caregiver intervention taxonomy and outcome-matching logic, while Walter and Pinquart 2020 remains useful as an integrated supporting meta-analysis.

## Evidence Limits

- Most included reviews were rated low quality by AMSTAR II, though review quality ratings did not clearly explain conclusions.
- Intervention categories and outcome labels were inconsistent across reviews.
- The same primary studies could be classified differently across reviews.
- Technology-based intervention evidence was difficult to interpret because medium and content were often confounded.
- This source is not evidence for dementia clinical treatment, AI-agent effectiveness, smart-home effectiveness, or chatbot effectiveness.
