# Conti 2019 Secondary Conditions and SCI Caregiver Burden

## Source Role

Conti et al. 2019 is a multicenter cross-sectional dyad study linking physical secondary conditions among people with SCI to dimensions of family caregiver burden. Use it for SCI caregiver burden mechanisms, secondary-condition prevention rationale, and assessment/evaluation fields. Do not use it as causal evidence, intervention-effectiveness evidence, or technology-effectiveness evidence.

## Study-Level Picture

- Problem/gap: SCI caregiver burden is recognized, but the relationship between specific physical and psychological secondary conditions of care recipients with SCI and multidimensional caregiver burden was unclear.
- Population/setting: 56 family caregiver and SCI care-recipient dyads recruited from outpatient clinics at spinal units in Turin, Italy and Dublin, Ireland.
- Inclusion/exclusion logic: Included adult family caregivers providing informal assistance to relatives with traumatic or nontraumatic SCI discharged at least 6 months from rehabilitation. Excluded formal paid caregivers and people with cognitive impairments or mixed diagnoses such as brain injury.
- Sample picture: Caregivers were mostly female, married/partnered, co-resident, and often spouses. Care recipients were mostly male, often had paraplegia, mostly traumatic SCI, and more than half had incomplete injury.
- Measures: Care-recipient functional independence with modified Barthel Index; physical secondary conditions with SCI Secondary Conditions Scale; quality of life with SF-8; depression with PHQ-9; anxiety with GAD-7. Caregiver burden with the Caregiver Burden Inventory in Spinal Cord Injury.
- Methods: Cross-sectional dyad survey with descriptive statistics, t-tests, and bivariate correlations.
- Key results: Caregiver burden was related to physical secondary conditions and functional independence, but not to care-recipient mental health measures in this sample.
- Specific condition findings: Bladder dysfunction and urinary tract infections were associated with multiple burden dimensions. Pressure injuries were associated especially with time-dependent burden. Chronic pain was weakly associated with developmental and emotional burden.
- Caregiving context findings: Higher burden was associated with tetraplegia, incomplete injury, more than 8 hours of daily care, more than 3 years of caregiving, and lower caregiver education.

## Wiki Interpretation

This study makes SCI secondary-condition prevention a dyadic caregiver-system concern. Bladder routines, UTIs, pressure-injury vigilance, chronic pain, and functional dependence can shape caregiver time, physical strain, emotional strain, and family burden. The study supports adding secondary-condition status and caregiver burden dimensions to SCI caregiver assessment and evaluation, while preserving the non-causal evidence boundary.

## Design Implications

- Include SCI secondary-condition fields in caregiver assessment: bladder dysfunction, UTIs, pressure injuries, chronic pain, respiratory/circulation issues, functional independence, caregiving hours, and years of caregiving.
- Treat pressure-injury prevention and bladder/UTI routines as possible time-burden and emotional-burden drivers.
- Pair care-recipient self-management support with caregiver psychoeducation and support.
- Evaluate caregiver burden dimensions separately rather than relying only on total burden.
- Do not claim that a digital, AI, or monitoring intervention reduces burden unless directly evaluated.

## Evidence Limits

- Cross-sectional correlations do not establish causality.
- Small sample limits generalizability and subgroup analysis.
- Participants came from two outpatient spinal units in Italy and Ireland.
- Caregiver participation was optional within a larger self-care scale-validation protocol.
- The null association between care-recipient psychological status and caregiver burden may reflect sample size or study design.
- This is not AI, smart-home, mHealth intervention-effectiveness, autonomous care, or SCI disease-background evidence.
