# Bressan 2020 Dementia Caregiver Needs Mixed-Method Review

## Source Context

Bressan, Visintini, and Palese 2020 is a mixed-method systematic review of family caregiver needs when caring for a person with dementia at home.

Original citation:

- `2020_bressan_dementia-caregiver-needs-mixed-method-review`: Bressan V, Visintini C, Palese A. What do family caregivers of people with dementia need? A mixed-method systematic review. Health and Social Care in the Community. 2020;28:1942-1960. DOI: `10.1111/hsc.13048`. Source file: `sources/papers/cg_system_core/14_bressan_2020_dementia_caregiver_needs_mixed_method.pdf`.

## Review Scope

- Search scope: PubMed, CINAHL, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, and PsycINFO.
- Publication window: 2009-2019.
- Included studies: 34 quantitative, qualitative, and mixed-method primary studies.
- Population: Adult family caregivers of people with dementia living at home.
- Exclusions: Caregivers for non-dementia conditions, care-recipient-only needs, nursing-home settings, professional/formal caregivers, underage caregivers, secondary reviews, dissertations, commentaries, letters, and editorials.
- Quality appraisal: Mixed-Methods Appraisal Tool, used to guide interpretation rather than exclude studies.

## Needs Taxonomy

The review synthesized four interrelated caregiver need themes:

- Being supported.
- Receiving accessible and personalized information.
- Being trained and educated to manage changes.
- Finding a balance.

## Source-Backed Findings

- Dementia caregiving at home is dynamic and requires continuous adjustment as disease symptoms, care tasks, and family roles change.
- Support needs included formal services, daily care assistance, respite, dementia-specific services, financial/legal help, psychological and emotional support, social support, and medical or mental healthcare assistance.
- Information needs included understandable, trusted, timely, and tailored information about dementia diagnosis, symptoms, trajectory, medications, adverse effects, behavioral issues, emergencies, services, home adaptation, incontinence, nutrition, financial/legal support, and care rights.
- Training needs included coping skills, care skills, communication with the person with dementia, nonpharmacological strategies for behavioral symptoms, safety and dignity, and future planning.
- Balance needs included time for oneself, maintenance of health and social relationships, respite, stability/control, acceptance of formal or informal help, and role adjustment.
- Caregiver needs intersect with care-recipient needs and service-system organization, but they should not be treated as identical.

## Review-Level Takeaway

Bressan 2020 is the current dementia-needs anchor. It supports trajectory-sensitive caregiver support that combines timely information, practical training, social/emotional support, service navigation, and self-care sustainability.

## Evidence Limits

- This is needs synthesis, not intervention-effectiveness evidence.
- Included studies were mostly from indexed English-language literature; grey literature was not searched.
- Most caregivers represented in included studies were women; caution is needed when generalizing to men and underrepresented caregiver groups.
- Needs were often inferred from problems or issues rather than directly elicited with validated need-assessment instruments.
- The source is dementia-specific and home-care focused; do not generalize to all caregiving contexts without additional evidence.
