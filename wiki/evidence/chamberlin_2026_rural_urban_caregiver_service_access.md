# Urban Versus Rural Differences in Services Requested, Received, and Interest Expressed Among Informal Caregivers of Older U.S. Veterans

## Source Role

This national survey separates service interest, search, and receipt among informal caregivers of older US veterans. It adds rural access context, not evidence that a digital referral intervention works.

## Study Picture

- The analytic sample included 511 informal caregivers assisting community-dwelling U.S. military veterans aged 65 or older: 258 rural and 253 urban, classified with Rural-Urban Commuting Area codes.
- Participants had provided assistance for more than one month, accompanied the veteran to at least one in-person medical appointment in the prior year, and were not professional caregivers.
- The survey assessed caregiver interest in, searching for, and receipt of nine home- and community-based service categories: caregiver support, community, exploitation/abuse/neglect, financial, helping, home safety/equipment, legal, respite, and transportation.
- The sample was deliberately balanced by rurality, used an 80/20 White/other-than-White recruitment target, and was recruited through an online Qualtrics panel.
- Service-specific search and receipt analyses were restricted to the 226 caregivers who first reported searching for any service (107 rural and 119 urban). Interest analyses used the full 511-person sample. Do not extrapolate receipt percentages to all caregivers.

## Main Findings

- Only 226 of 511 caregivers (44.2%) reported searching for any service; the proportion did not differ significantly by rurality.
- Service interest was similar across rural and urban caregivers. Leading interests were helping services (52.7% rural; 58.3% urban), caregiver support (48.6%; 52.4%), and home safety/equipment (46.7%; 52.4%).
- Among the 226 searchers, rural caregivers were more likely to search for respite; the reported urban-versus-rural odds ratio was 0.53 (95% CI 0.29-0.96; p=.0362).
- Among those 226 searchers, urban caregivers were more likely to report receiving caregiver support (OR 3.01, 95% CI 1.26-7.19), community services (OR 2.35, 95% CI 1.06-5.20), and helping services (OR 2.32, 95% CI 1.10-4.86).
- Respite receipt did not differ significantly (urban-versus-rural OR 0.85, 95% CI 0.41-1.76, p=.6659; Table 4). More rural respite searching should not be rewritten as a demonstrated rural respite-receipt deficit, even though the discussion describes respite access broadly as a gap.
- Rural and urban veterans were similar on measured ADL/IADL functioning, clinician count, medication count, and emergency visits, although the groups differed in several demographic characteristics.

## Evidence Limits

- Cross-sectional data cannot establish the temporal path from interest to search to receipt, and the survey did not link a specific searched service to subsequent receipt of that same service.
- The online panel may underrepresent caregivers with limited digital access or literacy and may partly explain the relatively young caregiver sample.
- The veteran population, VA-linked context, deliberate sampling quotas, and requirement for medical-appointment accompaniment limit generalizability.
- Rural-urban differences may reflect availability, staffing, distance, eligibility, awareness, and other unmeasured factors; the study does not test a digital or service-delivery intervention.
- Findings should inform access and implementation design, not be treated as evidence that a directory, telehealth tool, or referral app will close the service gap.

## Wiki Interpretation and Design Implications

Similar expressed interest in the full sample alongside lower reported rural receipt of selected services among searchers is consistent with structural access barriers, but the study does not identify their causes. Referral systems should be evaluated for completed service access, with availability, eligibility, staffing, transport, and offline assistance treated as testable constraints rather than assumed explanations.

These implications support research and design, not diagnosis, treatment recommendations, or autonomous care decisions.
