# Elliott 2009 Brief Problem-Solving Training for Recent-Onset SCI Caregivers

## Source Role

Elliott and Berry 2009 is SCI-specific randomized trial evidence for brief problem-solving training plus education during the first year after SCI. Use it as recent-onset SCI caregiver transition, problem-solving process, education-plus-support, and evaluation-boundary evidence.

## Full-Study Picture

- Problem/gap: Intervention evidence for family caregivers of people with SCI was sparse, especially for brief, low-cost support during the first year after injury.
- Population/condition/setting: 60 family caregivers of people with recent-onset SCI recruited from an inpatient SCI rehabilitation program in the southern United States.
- Care-recipient context: All care recipients had SCI within 12 months of study entry; 28 had paraplegia, 29 had tetraplegia, and 3 had spinal stenosis; 31 had complete lesions and 29 had incomplete lesions.
- Intervention/system: Brief individualized PST plus education, including an initial 2-3 hour session, follow-up at 6 and 12 months, educational materials as needed, and caregiver-requested telephone contacts or booster sessions.
- Comparator: Usual care control with baseline, 6-month, and 12-month evaluations and usual access to outpatient clinic staff and SCI-management information.
- Key innovation: A low-intensity, problem-tailored caregiver support model around discharge and first-year follow-up, using card-sort assessment to identify personally salient caregiver problems.
- Main measures: Social Problem-Solving Inventory-Revised, Inventory to Diagnose Depression, and SF-36 quality-of-life subscales at baseline, 6 months, and 12 months.
- Key results: The intervention significantly reduced dysfunctional problem-solving style scores over time, with a large effect size estimate. There were no observable effects on caregiver depression. Physical and social functioning trends approached significance with moderate effect sizes, but require caution.

## Wiki Interpretation

This source supports a first-year SCI caregiver transition support model centered on problem identification, problem-solving style, education, and lightweight follow-up. It should not be treated as evidence that brief PST improves caregiver depression.

## Design Implications

- Add first-year SCI caregiver assessment around personally salient problems, readiness, and follow-up fit.
- Separate problem-solving process outcomes from emotional health and quality-of-life outcomes.
- Treat caregiver-requested booster contact and educational materials as potentially useful support components, not as isolated PST effects.
- Evaluate whether brief supports reach caregivers who are under time, financial, and family-transition strain.

## Evidence Limits

- No observable caregiver depression effect.
- The active ingredient cannot be isolated because PST was bundled with education, information, and staff attention.
- Dropout and self-selection may affect generalizability.
- Physical and social functioning signals did not meet conventional statistical significance.
- This is not AI-agent, smart-home, SCI disease-treatment, or autonomous clinical decision evidence.
