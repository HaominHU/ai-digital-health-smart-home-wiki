# Graven 2021 Evidence on Telehealth Interventions for Family Caregivers

## Source Role

This systematic review analyzes randomized controlled trials of telehealth interventions for family caregivers of people with chronic health conditions. It is useful as broad caregiver telehealth intervention evidence and as a delivery-mode/outcome-domain anchor.

## Review-Level Takeaway

Graven 2021 is useful because it narrows caregiver telehealth evidence to randomized controlled trials and then organizes the evidence by telecommunication modality, intervention components, caregiver applications, user control, and caregiver outcomes. Its central contribution is not simply that "telehealth works"; it shows that telephone delivery was the dominant mode in the RCT evidence base, that skills training was the most common strategy across telephone, web, and combined telephone/web modalities, and that psychological functioning was the most frequent domain with significant improvement.

For this wiki, the practical takeaway is that remote caregiver support should be designed around concrete intervention functions such as skills training, psychoeducation, counseling, self-monitoring/tracking, reminders, and communication access. Telehealth delivery is a channel and implementation strategy; it is not by itself an AI, smart-home, or autonomous care model.

## Source-Backed Points

- The review searched 17 databases for English-language randomized controlled trials published from January 2002 to January 2017.
- Fifty-seven articles met inclusion criteria.
- Included studies had to use at least one telehealth component in 50% or more of caregiver training sessions and include at least one caregiver outcome.
- Telephone was the most frequently used telehealth delivery mode.
- Included studies focused heavily on caregivers of older adults with dementia and stroke, with additional chronic-condition contexts including HIV/AIDS, heart failure, psychiatric disability, traumatic brain injury, cancer, hospice/palliative care, and pediatric conditions.
- Skills training was the most common treatment strategy across telephone, web, and combined telephone/web interventions.
- Improved psychological functioning was the most frequently reported significant caregiver outcome.
- The review concludes that telehealth can deliver caregiver interventions and can improve several target caregiver outcomes across studies.
- Further research is needed on caregiving skills, caregiver self-efficacy, caregiver health outcomes, comparisons with in-person delivery, and underserved or rural caregiver populations.

## Included Technology and Intervention Taxonomy

The review included telecommunication-based RCTs using Internet, telephone, videophone, point-to-point videoconference, and/or chat. It organized eligible studies mainly into telephone, web, and combined telephone/web modalities.

Intervention components included:

- Group or individual skills training.
- Psychoeducational and resource materials.
- Self-monitoring and tracking.
- Reminders.
- Group or individual counseling.

Caregiver applications included email, discussion forums or online chat, online journaling, text messaging, telephone calls outside the core intervention, and useful links or resources. User control was coded as facilitator-guided when professionals led sessions or maintained participation flow, and self-guided when participants completed the program without facilitator guidance.

## Population and Condition Scope

The included RCTs focused heavily on caregivers of older adults with dementia and stroke. Additional chronic-condition contexts included HIV/AIDS, heart failure, psychiatric disability, traumatic brain injury, cancer, hospice and palliative care, and pediatric conditions. Spinal cord injury relevance is cautious and indirect because the review included SCI caregiver problem-solving or videoconferencing trials but was not a dedicated SCI caregiver synthesis.

Older-adult relevance should be mapped through dementia, stroke, frailty, hospice/palliative care, or care context, not by treating disease or disability as normal aging.

## Outcome and Evidence-Quality Taxonomy

The review examined caregiver outcomes such as psychosocial functioning, health status, health behaviors, caregiving abilities, service utilization, cost, and clinical outcomes. Psychological functioning was the most frequent significant improvement domain. The review found a weaker or less developed evidence base for caregiving skills, caregiver self-efficacy, and caregiver health outcomes.

The RCT-only inclusion strengthens causal relevance compared with mixed-design reviews, but the authors did not conduct a formal meta-analysis because populations, interventions, modalities, and outcomes were heterogeneous. That makes this source useful for intervention and evaluation design, but not for estimating pooled effect sizes.

## Wiki Interpretation

For this wiki, this source supports telehealth as a caregiver intervention delivery lens. It should inform modality selection, access/equity reasoning, and outcome planning for caregiver digital health interventions.

## Evidence Limits

- The review did not conduct a formal meta-analysis because studies were heterogeneous.
- It supports telehealth caregiver intervention evidence, not AI-agent, smart-home, or general digital health effectiveness claims.
- Psychological functioning has the strongest broad signal; caregiving skills, self-efficacy, and caregiver health outcomes require more evidence.
- Broad chronic-condition generalization should be cautious because dementia and stroke caregiver studies were prominent.
- Do not use this review as direct care-recipient clinical outcome evidence.

## What This Review Does Not Answer

- Whether AI agents, smart-home systems, passive sensing, or autonomous monitoring are effective caregiver interventions.
- Which telehealth modality is best for a specific condition or caregiver subgroup.
- Whether web or combined telephone/web interventions perform as well as matched in-person interventions; the source notes that few studies tested matched in-person comparators.
- Whether telehealth improves care-recipient clinical outcomes independent of caregiver outcomes.
- How to implement telehealth for underserved or rural caregivers at scale, beyond identifying this as a future research need.
