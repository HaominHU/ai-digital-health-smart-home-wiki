# Digital access and frailty in older adults: A moderated mediation model of digital literacy and social support

## Source Role

This South Korean survey analysis distinguishes device access, home connectivity, task-based digital literacy, and available support in relation to prefrail/frail status. It does not evaluate a digital intervention or caregivers.

## Study Picture

- The study analyzed 6,625 community-dwelling adults aged 65 or older from the 2023 National Survey of Older Koreans.
- Participants with physician-diagnosed dementia (n=205), suspected dementia based on a K-MMSE-2 score of 23 or lower (n=3,171), or missing digital-literacy data (n=77) were excluded.
- Digital access had three categories: no smartphone/computer, a smartphone/computer without home internet, and a smartphone/computer with home internet.
- Digital literacy was a 13-item count of tasks performed independently. Social support measured the number of available people providing emotional, practical, or financial help.
- Frailty risk combined prefrail and frail categories on the FRAIL scale into a binary outcome.
- Survey-weighted generalized structural equation models accounted for weights, strata, and primary sampling units, with adjustment for demographic, socioeconomic, residential, lifestyle, BMI, cognitive-function, and depressive-symptom variables.

## Main Findings

- Weighted estimates indicated 13.4% had no personal device, 32.1% had a device without home internet, and 54.5% had connected device access. The mean digital-literacy score was 5.61 of 13.
- The sample was mostly robust (75.1%); 23.3% were prefrail and 1.6% frail.
- Connected device access was associated with lower odds of prefrail/frail status than no personal device (reported OR 0.71, reported 95% CI 0.53-0.89) and than device access without home internet (reported OR 0.78, reported 95% CI 0.64-0.93). Device access without home internet did not differ significantly from no device access. These are Table 2 values as printed, subject to the reporting caveat below.
- Digital access was associated with higher digital literacy, and digital literacy was associated with lower frailty risk. Indirect associations through digital literacy were statistically significant for all three access contrasts; direct access-frailty associations were not significant after accounting for literacy.
- Only one of nine moderated-mediation contrasts was significant: practical support for connected access versus no personal device (reported index -0.019, 95% CI -0.036 to -0.003, p=.019). Emotional and financial support did not significantly moderate the indirect associations; practical support should not be generalized across all access contrasts.

## Evidence Limits and Conceptual Boundaries

- The cross-sectional design cannot establish temporal sequence, mediation as a causal mechanism, or that improving access/literacy will prevent frailty.
- The cognitively selected sample and very low frailty prevalence limit transferability to people with dementia, cognitive impairment, or higher support needs.
- Access measures did not capture device quality, affordability, stability, actual use, usage purpose, or digital-health literacy. Social support was a count rather than a validated multidimensional scale.
- South Korea's infrastructure, social-support patterns, and care systems may not generalize to other contexts.
- Frailty is a clinical/functional syndrome, not normal aging. Digital exclusion should be treated as a potentially modifiable structural correlate, not as a diagnosis or sole cause of frailty.
- Source reporting is internally inconsistent: Table 2 gives an access-to-literacy coefficient of 2.470 where the narrative gives 2.417, and some total-effect coefficients/p-values also differ. Moreover, Table 2's reported OR confidence limits do not exactly equal exponentiation of its printed log-odds limits despite the table note (for example, -0.591 to -0.091 exponentiates to about 0.554-0.913, not the printed 0.53-0.89). Values above are retained as reported, not independently validated or silently corrected. Verify with the authors or a correction before precision-dependent quantitative reuse.

## Wiki Interpretation and Design Implications

Digital inclusion research should measure access, connectivity, skills, practical support, and meaningful use separately. Coaching and digital-navigation support are plausible interventions to test, not proven frailty-prevention mechanisms. The cognitive exclusions preclude direct transfer to dementia caregiving.

These implications support research and design, not diagnosis, treatment recommendations, or autonomous care decisions.
