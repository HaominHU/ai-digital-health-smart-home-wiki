# Health Literacy of caregivers of older adults with Alzheimer's disease: a qualitative study

## Source Role

Documents seeking, understanding, communicating, and assessing information in Alzheimer family caregiving. Information availability and formal education did not remove practical information difficulties in this selected sample.

## Study Picture

- Twelve unpaid primary family caregivers of older adults with diagnosed AD were recruited through a WhatsApp-mediated support group in Arapiraca, Alagoas, Brazil. Interviews took place at participants' homes in April-May 2024, averaged about 30 minutes, and were analyzed by two researchers.
- Ten caregivers were women; ages ranged from 23 to 56. Nine had completed higher education; eight were adult children. All co-resided with the older adult, and all care recipients used private health services. This is not a representative low-literacy, low-income, or digitally excluded sample.
- Findings are organized around seeking, understanding, communicating, and assessing information. Ten reported searching for disease information; internet and social platforms were important sources. Difficulties included translating diagnosis/medication-focused guidance into everyday behavioral care, relying on family members to interpret guidance, and judging the credibility and applicability of online claims.
- The accounts also illustrate mistaken interpretation of dementia symptoms as normal aging. The wiki should describe this as a reported misconception, never as a biological equivalence.

## Evidence Limits and Interpretation

The study supports a distinction between information availability, practical comprehension, family communication, and critical appraisal. It does not measure population prevalence, validate a health-literacy score, test an educational intervention, or establish that online-group participation improves outcomes. Recruitment through one support group, a relatively educated/private-care sample, absent participant feedback, and no methodological triangulation limit transferability.

**Interpretation:** information support should address all four activities, practical application, and critical appraisal rather than assuming educational attainment ensures those abilities. Source verification cues and human help are research/design implications; AI fact-checking effectiveness was not tested.

PDF locators: methods pp. 3-4; sample and findings pp. 5-10; final considerations pp. 10-11. Integrate generalized findings, not participant pseudonyms or individual narratives.
