# Hartnett 2016 Caregiver Burden in End-Stage Ovarian Cancer

## Source Summary

Hartnett et al. 2016 is a descriptive, cross-sectional pilot study of caregiver burden among primary caregivers of patients with end-stage ovarian cancer at an urban comprehensive cancer center. Fifty caregivers completed an anonymous voluntary survey using the Caregiver Reaction Assessment.

## Key Evidence

- Eligible patients had end-stage ovarian cancer and Karnofsky Performance Scale scores of 30 or less.
- Caregivers included spouses, significant others, children, and close friends.
- Caregiver Reaction Assessment subscales included disrupted schedules, financial problems, lack of family support, health problems, and caregiver self-esteem.
- Caregivers endorsed self-esteem most strongly, indicating pride, enjoyment, or feeling privileged in caregiving.
- Disrupted schedules and financial problems were the most burdensome domains.
- Caregiver self-esteem did not significantly correlate with negative burden subscales, suggesting positive meaning can coexist with practical strain.
- Lower income and lower education were associated with greater financial burden in this sample.
- Higher-income, unemployed, or retired caregivers reported greater schedule disruption in this sample.
- The authors recommend caregiver preparation, symptom-management education, interdisciplinary support, social work referral, financial and medication assistance, support groups, and respite.

## Wiki-Relevant Interpretation

This source provides end-stage ovarian cancer caregiver burden evidence. It should support the gynecological cancer condition overlay but should remain explicitly bounded to end-stage ovarian cancer and a small pilot sample.

## Design Implications

- Advanced ovarian cancer caregiver support should include schedule support, financial/resource navigation, symptom-management education, self-care support, respite prompts, and referral pathways.
- Caregiver assessment should include both positive meaning/esteem and practical burden domains.
- Digital caregiver tools should not assume that pride in caregiving reduces schedule, financial, health, or support burdens.

## Gaps and Cautions

- Convenience sample of 50 caregivers at one urban comprehensive cancer center.
- Cross-sectional design; associations should not be interpreted causally.
- English literacy was required.
- Sample was mostly Caucasian, partnered/married, and college educated.
- The study recommends nurse-led support and future intervention research but does not evaluate an intervention.
