# An App for Care Partners of Persons Living With Dementia (Aliviado Caregiving): Human-Centered Design Study

## Source Role

Aliviado Caregiving adds formative evidence for caregiver-priority-sensitive BPSD support and explainable, contestable recommendations. It evaluates co-design rather than algorithm performance or caregiver outcomes.

## Study and System Picture

- Stage 1 combined investigator-team requirements with a 2020 convenience survey of 101 clinicians who had used the clinician-facing Aliviado app. Most clinicians were nurses (45/101) and White (75/101).
- Stage 2 used virtual think-aloud review with eight English-speaking adult dementia care partners who owned smartphones and provided at least four hours of weekly care. Participants were four White, three Black/African American, and one Asian; demographic response was optional.
- Stage 3 involved a nine-member care partner advisory board recruited with an African American faith-community leader. All nine identified as Black/African American; five attended a focus group and all nine participated in individual virtual meetings.
- The caregiver-facing app was designed to help care partners prioritize behavioral and psychological symptoms of dementia (BPSD), complete BPSD/stress assessment, and select evidence-based nonpharmacological strategies.
- A machine-learning prioritization engine could recommend a symptom priority different from the care partner's stated priority. Care partners could retain their own priority or accept the algorithmic recommendation.

## Main Findings

- Clinicians most often prioritized caregiver stress-management strategies (85/101, 84%), dementia symptom-management education/resources (74/101, 73%), caregiver stress self-monitoring (70/101, 69%), and BPSD monitoring/tracking (60/101, 59%).
- Two qualitative categories were facilitators of care partner engagement and desired features for a dementia caregiving app.
- Engagement-supporting design included progress feedback, reflective journaling, personalized names/relationship terms, tailored guidance, and flexible prioritization.
- Advisory-board members wanted a clear rationale when the algorithm's recommendation differed from caregiver judgment. This is direct human-centered evidence for explainability, contestability, and respect for caregiver experiential knowledge.
- Desired future functions included continuously updated evidence, Spanish-language support, support for multiple care recipients, and participation by multiple care partners or professional aides.
- The faith-community partnership illustrates a trusted-messenger route for engaging a population often underrepresented in digital health design.

## Evidence Limits and Safety Boundaries

- The study evaluates a design process, wireframes, and a beta app; it does not establish usability scores, sustained use, feasibility in daily care, BPSD improvement, burden reduction, health equity effects, or effectiveness.
- Care partner samples were small, representation varied by stage, Latino care partners were not included, and care partners were not involved from the absolute inception of the broader Aliviado program.
- All feedback was virtual; real-world workflow, crisis conditions, access barriers, and longitudinal engagement remain untested.
- The recommendation component's accuracy, calibration, failure modes, subgroup performance, and safety were not evaluated.
- BPSD prioritization must remain decision support. It requires human oversight, source transparency, uncertainty communication, urgent-risk escalation, and explicit limits against diagnosis, treatment selection, or autonomous clinical triage.
- The paper discloses that an author created the app and that New York University owns the Aliviado program and intellectual property.

## Wiki Interpretation and Design Implications

When a suggested symptom priority differs from caregiver judgment, explain the rationale and preserve the ability to disagree. The community partnership informs recruitment and coproduction design; it does not establish equitable outcomes. Multilingual and multi-caregiver features remain requested enhancements unless separately verified as implemented.

These implications support research and design, not diagnosis, treatment recommendations, or autonomous care decisions.
