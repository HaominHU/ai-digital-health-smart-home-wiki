# Multimorbidity and Overlapping Needs

## Core Idea

The wiki should support cases where aging-related functional decline coexists with chronic disease, disability, cancer, dementia, or other conditions.

## Operating Rule

Do not force a single-cause explanation when multiple conditions, impairments, treatments, medications, environments, and caregiving constraints may interact.

## Analysis Questions

- Which needs are shared across conditions?
- Which needs are condition-specific?
- Which needs come from caregiving role demands?
- Which needs come from the care recipient's condition, disability, symptoms, function, or environment?
- Which needs are shaped by age-associated prevalence or later-life disease development?

## Evidence

- `2015_aneshensel-avison_stress-process-appreciation-pearlin`: Stress proliferation provides conceptual support for tracking interacting and cascading stressors across role, family, work, economic, and health domains rather than forcing a single-cause explanation.
- `2016_nasem_families-caring-for-an-aging-america`: The report identifies high-need older adults as those with probable dementia or substantial self-care limitations and situates caregiver demand within chronic health problems, functional limitation, cognitive impairment, family structure, work, and LTSS context.
- `2020_schulz_family-caregiving-for-older-adults`: The review notes that high-need/high-cost older adults can include people with multiple chronic diseases plus functional limitations, probable dementia, or end-of-life care needs, and that these contexts increase risk for unmet care needs.

## Contextual Attribution in Dementia Design

Nyamathi stakeholders requested comorbidity, medication, functional, and life-history context in persona/DAM artifacts. Their requests reinforce attention to interacting causes and avoiding attribution of every behavior to dementia; they are formative design evidence, not validated symptom attribution or clinical decision rules.
