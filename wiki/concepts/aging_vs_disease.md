# Aging vs Disease

## Core Distinction

Aging-related functional decline, chronic disease, disability, and age-associated disease prevalence must be distinguished in this wiki.

## Operating Rule

Do not treat a condition as normal aging simply because it is common among older adults.

## Examples

- Dementia in later life is not normal aging.
- Gynecological cancer among older adults is a disease condition that may be age-associated or more prevalent with aging.
- Acquired spinal cord injury in a younger adult should not be treated as the same problem as aging-related mobility or spinal function decline.
- Falls can result from aging-related functional decline, disease-specific impairment, treatment side effects, medication burden, home environment mismatch, or multiple interacting causes.

## Evidence

- `2016_nasem_families-caring-for-an-aging-america`: The report states that need for help is not an inevitable consequence of aging and is driven by physical, cognitive, mental health, and chronic health or functional limitations.
- `2020_schulz_family-caregiving-for-older-adults`: The review frames older-adult caregiving in relation to chronic illness, disability, dementia, multimorbidity, and care complexity rather than treating later-life conditions as normal aging.
