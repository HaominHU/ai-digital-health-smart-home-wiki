# Caregiver Activation

## Core Definition

Caregiver activation is a multidimensional, dynamic state of readiness that makes a caregiver willing and able to learn, adapt, collaborate, and act. It can begin before formal caregiving and can change as care demands, health, burden, resources, relationships, and system support change.

## Knowledge-Network Model

| Dimension | What it includes | Support implication |
|---|---|---|
| Care capabilities | Care management and caregiver self-care knowledge and skills | Provide tailored education, practice, tools, and health support |
| Care autonomy | Initiating and organizing care while recognizing when help is needed | Enable action without transferring professional responsibility |
| Care resilience | Sustaining adaptation and care capacity under recurring stress | Strengthen coping resources, respite, support, and recovery |
| Self-efficacy | Confidence in tasks, communication, collaboration, and decisions | Use coached practice, feedback, and role-specific support |

Communication connects capabilities, autonomy, and self-efficacy. Antecedents include care-recipient dependency, the caregiver's intermediary role, insufficient proficiency, and caregiver burden. Potential consequences include service access or use, caregiver well-being, and care-management behavior, but causal pathways remain unproven.

## Distinctions

- **Activation is not empowerment.** Activation centers on readiness and capability; empowerment centers on influence, agency, shared decisions, and power. Activation may support empowerment but does not guarantee it.
- **Activation is not self-efficacy.** Self-efficacy is one component.
- **Activation is not health literacy.** Health literacy does not capture the full behavioral, adaptive, motivational, and relational construct.
- **Autonomy is not abandonment.** A caregiver who acts independently must still have professional collaboration and escalation routes.
- **Low activation is not blame.** It may reflect burden, inadequate training, inaccessible information, poor collaboration, caregiver health, resource scarcity, or technology demands.

## Operational Use

Use activation as a longitudinal assessment and intervention-tailoring lens. Measure its dimensions separately where possible, pair caregiver self-report with contextual variables, and distinguish activation from caregiver workload and outcomes. Never use an activation score to justify shifting clinical decisions or surveillance responsibility to a family caregiver.

## Evidence

- `2026-07-24_hong_caregiver-activation-concept-analysis`: Walker and Avant concept analysis based on 51 studies identified four defining attributes, four antecedents, three consequence domains, and several candidate empirical referents. It clarifies the construct but does not validate a universal measure or establish causal effects.

## Gaps

- Cross-condition and cross-cultural measurement validity.
- Sensitivity to change over the caregiving trajectory.
- Whether interventions change activation and whether that mediates meaningful caregiver, care-process, or care-recipient outcomes.
- How activation interacts with structural access, clinical partnership, burden, disability, language, digital literacy, and technology design.
