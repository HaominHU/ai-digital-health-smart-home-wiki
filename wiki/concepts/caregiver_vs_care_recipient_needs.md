# Caregiver vs Care Recipient Needs

## Core Distinction

The wiki must separately track caregiver needs and care recipient needs, even when the same technology or event affects both.

## Caregiver Needs

Caregiver needs may include coordination, monitoring burden, emotional load, decision support, respite, training, safety awareness, and managing uncertainty.

## Care Recipient Needs

Care recipient needs may include autonomy, safety, function, privacy, dignity, symptom management, access, communication, and support in daily activities.

## Operating Rule

When ingesting or querying a source, explicitly ask:

- What is the caregiver need?
- What is the care recipient need?
- Where do they overlap?
- Where might they conflict?
- If a source reports patient or care-recipient preferences, should those preferences be treated separately from caregiver preferences?

## Evidence

- `2008_schulz-sherwood_physical-mental-health-effects-family-caregiving`: Care recipient disability, cognitive impairment, behavior problems, suffering, and care intensity can become caregiver stressors and health risks, so care-recipient needs and caregiver outcomes should be linked but not collapsed.
- `2011-11-22_van-houtven_organizing-framework-informal-caregiver-interventions`: Caregiver interventions should distinguish caregiving activities, caregiver outcomes, and care-recipient outcomes.
- `2016_nasem_families-caring-for-an-aging-america`: Person- and family-centered care requires engaging family caregivers while preserving care-recipient autonomy, privacy, and dignity.
- `2020_schulz_family-caregiving-for-older-adults`: Caregiving can affect caregiver well-being, social relationships, work, and health while care-recipient needs may involve function, symptoms, safety, and suffering; these related concerns should not be collapsed.
- `2026_kingsada_preferences-digital-health-technologies`: Patient preferences for digital health technologies should not be assumed to equal caregiver preferences, especially when systems involve shared access, proxy use, privacy trade-offs, or caregiver-mediated technology use.
