# Digital Inclusion and Supported Use

## Core Distinction

Device ownership, reliable connectivity, digital skills, actual use, practical assistance, and meaningful outcomes are distinct. Providing a device does not establish usable access; usable access does not establish benefit. This page owns that distinction across technologies, while digital distress owns technology-related stress and the formal-service-access hub owns access to human services.

## Evidence-Backed Anchors

- Hwang and Yi's South Korean older-adult survey distinguishes device access with and without home internet, task-based literacy, and available support. Connected access and literacy were associated with prefrail/frail status; only the practical-support contrast for connected access versus no device had a significant moderated-mediation index. Cross-sectional mediation is not a demonstrated causal pathway. The sample excluded diagnosed and suspected dementia, and numerical reporting discrepancies limit precise reuse. See `wiki/evidence/hwang_2026_digital_access_literacy_frailty.md`.
- Tong's Chinese survey analysis distinguishes ownership breadth and device function. It does not measure actual use or show that ownership prevents depression; the any-ownership fixed-effects estimate was not significant. Functional limitations, care need, and device selection complicate interpretation. See `wiki/evidence/tong_2026_smart_device_ownership_depressive_symptoms.md`.
- Kolakowski's multicountry platform pilot identifies connectivity, device overload, interface complexity, and training barriers. Feasibility does not establish health benefit. See `wiki/evidence/kolakowski_2026_digital_health_intrinsic_capacity_feasibility.md`.
- Pemberton's connected-care study makes shared setup, family support, permissions, and reliability visible in adoption. It does not establish that caregiver mediation is always needed or beneficial. See `wiki/evidence/pemberton_2026_smart_home_connected_care_adoption.md`.

## Synthesis and Design Implications

The following is a research framework inferred across sources, not a validated causal sequence:

1. Material access: appropriate device, affordability, repair, and availability.
2. Connected access: reliable connectivity where required and usable fallback when unavailable.
3. Skills and comprehension: ability to perform relevant tasks and interpret outputs, not merely confidence or ownership.
4. Supported use: consented, practical help with setup, troubleshooting, and daily operation.
5. Function and context fit: alignment with a person's goals, functional abilities, routines, language, and preferences.
6. Outcomes: measure actual use, workload, autonomy, participation, and intended health or care outcomes separately.

Do not assume each stage causes the next. Digital navigators, coaching, low-bandwidth alternatives, and function-matched devices are design hypotheses to test. Supporter workload and cost must be measured rather than assigned to family members by default.

## Fit and Implementation Across Layers

Liu's older-adult study operationalizes perceived fit across physical, community, social, and digital conditions, while Batchelor's guideline development adds staff, provider, and client implementation perspectives. Long's review links individual, technical, social, and program factors to five derived design dimensions. This extends the inclusion framework to technology demands and organizational support; it does not validate a causal sequence from access to better health. See `wiki/evidence/liu_2026_person_environment_fit_aging_in_place.md`, `wiki/evidence/batchelor_2026_digital_technology_in_home_aged_care_guidelines.md`, and `wiki/evidence/long_2026_age_friendly_medication_adherence_dht_review.md`.

Assess readiness, ability, preference, access, actual use, and benefit separately. Client ownership does not establish health-tool use, and staff confidence does not establish client acceptance. Setup, training, and continuing assistance should be resourced and evaluated without assigning the work to family members by default.

## Literacy Within Existing Users

Tu's CHD survey adds variation in reported information acquisition/evaluation, interaction, and application among people who can already use digital devices. This complements access and fit evidence from other source lanes: device use does not establish literacy, and reported literacy does not establish observed task performance or sustained benefit. Its sample-derived profiles require external replication, classification-uncertainty handling, and comparison with continuous scores before being used for support matching. The study excluded nonusers and did not test tailored education, family assistance, or clinical outcomes. Source reporting caveats remain in `wiki/evidence/tu_2026_digital_health_literacy_profiles_coronary_heart_disease.md`.

**Design hypothesis:** evaluate task performance and support needs alongside self-report, include people unable to use current devices, and measure the effects of assistance rather than assigning it from profile labels. Profiles should not become fixed identities, automatic access restrictions, or reasons to blame users for structural barriers.

## Population and Safety Boundaries

- Older-adult observational findings are not caregiver intervention effects or direct dementia evidence.
- Frailty and depressive symptoms are not normal aging, and association does not establish diagnosis, prevention, or treatment benefit.
- Preserve independent use where feasible; needing assistance is not permission for blanket proxy access.
- Assistance requires granular consent, minimum necessary access, revocation, secure storage, and auditable sharing. Avoid exposing credentials or sensitive household and health data to informal helpers.
- Digital exclusion and digital distress can coexist but are not interchangeable. Hwang and Tong did not directly validate a digital-distress measure.

## Attitudes, Information Appraisal, and Sustained Use

Silva adds that digital participation can coexist with difficulty understanding and evaluating health information. Vandelli adds favorable perceived usefulness/willingness alongside low reported use; its latent classes infer trust from adoption-related proxies, not a validated trust scale. Xianheng excludes smartphone nonusers and people unable to complete surveys, limiting inclusive requirements claims. See `wiki/evidence/silva_2026_health_literacy_alzheimer_family_caregivers.md`, `wiki/evidence/vandelli_2026_trust_digital_health_remote_older_adults.md`, and `wiki/evidence/xianheng_2026_smart_home_care_platform_requirements_kano.md`.

As **adjacent patient-only evidence**, Svendsen distinguishes 18-month intervention access from active use and outcomes: median active days during months 6-18 were zero, and neither app had a statistically significant overall advantage over usual care. These different populations/designs do not establish a causal chain from readiness to use to benefit. See `wiki/evidence/svendsen_2026_digital_stress_management_breast_cancer.md`.

## Open Questions

- Which forms of practical assistance improve sustained meaningful use without increasing family workload or dependency?
- Do inclusion interventions change outcomes when cognition, function, income, baseline need, and technology selection are accounted for?
- Which accessibility and support measures transfer across countries, care systems, languages, and disabilities?
