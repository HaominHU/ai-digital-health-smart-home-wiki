## Why This Source Matters

This National Academies consensus report is a foundational policy and evidence synthesis for U.S. family caregiving of older adults. It anchors caregiver definitions, prevalence estimates, role complexity, person- and family-centered care, caregiver assessment, health/LTSS integration, economic support, and technology innovation.

## Supported Claims or Knowledge Roles

- Family caregivers include relatives, partners, neighbors, and friends whose involvement is driven mainly by personal relationship rather than payment.
- Need for caregiving is driven by functional limitation, cognitive impairment, mental health, and chronic health problems, not by age alone.
- In 2011, the report estimated 17.7 million U.S. family caregivers of older adults in community or residential care settings other than nursing homes, including 8.5 million caring for high-need older adults.
- High-need older adults include those with probable dementia or needing help with at least two self-care activities.
- Family caregivers increasingly provide medical care at home, navigate fragmented health and LTSS systems, interact with providers, and serve as surrogate decision makers.
- Caregiver support should move from person-centered care alone toward person- and family-centered care while preserving care-recipient autonomy and privacy.

## Evidence Limits

The report focuses on U.S. older-adult caregiving and U.S. health/LTSS policy. Time-sensitive policy details should be re-verified before current policy writing. Technology references are innovation and policy rationale, not direct evidence that a specific AI, smart home, or remote monitoring system is effective.
