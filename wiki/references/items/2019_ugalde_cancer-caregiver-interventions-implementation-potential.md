## Why This Source Matters

This systematic review appraises cancer caregiver intervention studies for implementation potential using early-stage Proctor implementation outcomes. It is useful for distinguishing evidence of intervention efficacy from evidence that a caregiver intervention is acceptable, adoptable, appropriate, feasible, deliverable with fidelity, and resourced for practice.

## Supported Claims or Knowledge Roles

- Cancer caregiver intervention studies often underreport implementation-relevant design and delivery details.
- Fewer than half of included studies reported caregiver-perspective acceptability evidence, and direct caregiver involvement in intervention development was rare.
- No included studies reported intentions, agreements, or actions to implement interventions in practice.
- Many eligible caregivers did not enroll, so completion among enrolled caregivers should not be treated as population reach.
- Implementation-ready caregiver interventions need clearer reporting of target subgroup, adoption partner, staff time, training, resources, fidelity, and cost.

## Evidence Limits

This is broad cancer caregiver implementation-readiness evidence, not gynecological-cancer-specific effectiveness evidence. It appraises implementation potential from published trial reporting and does not prove that interventions were never implemented after publication. Do not treat it as AI, smart-home, mHealth effectiveness, autonomous-care, cancer-treatment, or clinical decision-support evidence.
