## Why This Source Matters

This dissertation is a foundational source for the wiki's caregiver mHealth architecture, including modular support, caregiver self-care, training, condition-sensitive content, care coordination, usability, and future AI or wearable directions.

## Supported Claims or Knowledge Roles

- Family caregivers are direct intervention recipients with their own self-care, training, information, and coordination needs.
- Modular caregiver support can combine reusable and condition-specific components.
- The pilot supports usability and feasibility learning, not clinical effectiveness.

## Evidence Limits

The dissertation's focus group, co-design, and pilot samples are small. It does not establish clinical effectiveness or evaluate the proposed future AI, wearable, passive-monitoring, or clinician-workflow extensions.

## Privacy or Sensitivity Notes

No participant identifiers are retained. Downstream system designs require consent, role-based access, secure storage, auditability, and separate care-recipient autonomy controls.
