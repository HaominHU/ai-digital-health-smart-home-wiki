## Why This Source Matters

This source provides broad patient-preference evidence for digital health technologies. It helps the wiki distinguish adoption, acceptability, preference elicitation, and health-economics/HTA evidence from clinical effectiveness or caregiver intervention evidence.

## Supported Claims or Knowledge Roles

- Patient preferences for DHTs commonly involve cost, privacy, convenience, ease of use, utility, data security, personalization, clinician interaction, feedback, and hybrid digital/in-person support.
- Preference evidence is heterogeneous across DHT type, condition, age, user group, country, reimbursement context, and healthcare system.
- Quantitative, qualitative, and mixed-methods approaches provide complementary preference evidence.
- Discrete choice experiments dominate quantitative preference studies, while interviews and focus groups are common qualitative methods.
- Older-adult DHT preference evidence suggests value in simple, clearly explained, tailored tools, multimodal education, initial face-to-face interaction, privacy, comfort, and clinical support.
- Preference-sensitive digital health design should not be treated as evidence of clinical effectiveness or caregiver outcome effectiveness.
