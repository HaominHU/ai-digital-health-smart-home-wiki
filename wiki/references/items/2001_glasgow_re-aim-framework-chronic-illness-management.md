## Why This Source Matters

This source anchors evaluation of chronic illness management interventions across Reach, Efficacy, Adoption, Implementation, and Maintenance rather than efficacy alone. It is useful for digital health, smart-home, caregiver-support, and chronic-care intervention planning because it connects individual-level effects with real-world delivery and organizational uptake.

## Supported Claims or Knowledge Roles

- Intervention impact should be evaluated across reach, efficacy, adoption, implementation, and maintenance.
- Strong efficacy among participants can still yield limited population impact if reach, adoption, implementation, or maintenance are weak.
- Delivery modalities differ in barriers, scalability, consistency, adoption burden, and sustainability.
- Technology-enabled approaches may improve some RE-AIM dimensions but still require access, usability, workflow fit, and maintenance evaluation.
- Policy and system-level changes can shape chronic illness management through broad reach and sustained context change.

## Evidence Limits

- This is a framework and consensus-rating article, not a systematic meta-analysis of each intervention modality.
- It discusses chronic illness management generally, not family caregiving specifically.
- Technology-access assumptions reflect the 2000-era context and require current evidence before making present-day access claims.
- It should not be used as clinical guidance or as evidence that a specific AI, mHealth, or smart-home intervention is effective.
