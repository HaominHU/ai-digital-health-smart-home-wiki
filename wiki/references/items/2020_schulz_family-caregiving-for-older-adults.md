## Why This Source Matters

This review is a broad anchor for family caregiving among older adults with chronic illness and disability. It frames caregiving as a chronic stress exposure, summarizes caregiver role complexity and health impacts, and reviews intervention evidence including technology-based caregiver supports.

## Supported Claims or Knowledge Roles

- Family caregivers are a primary support source for older adults with chronic illness and disability.
- Caregiving roles have increased in scale, duration, intensity, and complexity.
- Caregiver tasks often accumulate across a trajectory from sporadic support to IADL/ADL help, medical tasks, care coordination, end-of-life care, and bereavement.
- Caregiver outcomes vary; risk is shaped by care intensity, care recipient suffering, behavioral symptoms, support availability, caregiver health, perceived choice, and home context.
- Technology-based caregiver interventions are feasible and acceptable in some studies, but sustained use, accessibility, cost-effectiveness, system design, and care integration remain concerns.

## Evidence Limits

This is a review synthesis, not a single effectiveness trial. Dementia caregiver evidence should not be generalized to all caregiving contexts without qualification. Technology-based intervention claims should be framed as feasibility, acceptability, and design rationale unless a specific trial supports stronger conclusions.
