## Why This Source Matters

Maps medication-support technologies, older-user barriers, and age-friendly design directions. The review offers a framework and evidence-gap map without validating the framework or estimating a pooled intervention effect.

## Supported Claims or Knowledge Roles

- Medication-support research is concentrated on daily routines, with limited coverage of other journey stages.
- Standardized usability, vulnerable subgroups, and bidirectional clinical integration are gaps within this review.
- The five age-friendly design dimensions are derived recommendations; they require validation and do not establish effectiveness.

## Evidence Limits

- The scoping synthesis includes secondary reviews and heterogeneous original studies without quality weighting or meta-analysis; do not treat the 60-source map as independent comparative effectiveness evidence.
- The five design dimensions are derived recommendations, not a validated scale, proven intervention components, or verified causal mechanisms. Emotional warmth or human-like check-ins do not establish superiority of AI interaction or substitutes for human support.
- Gray literature was not systematically searched, missing information was not requested from authors, and cost received limited analysis. Sparse vulnerable-subgroup evidence limits generalization to people living alone, with cognitive impairment, or in low-resource settings.
- Medication adherence is a care-recipient outcome; caregiver workload, preferences, and consent were not demonstrated. Chronic disease and polypharmacy are not normal aging, and this broad review is not specific evidence for each priority condition.
