## Why This Source Matters

This systematic review provides advanced cancer caregiver intervention taxonomy and outcome-mapping evidence. It is useful for distinguishing individual, dyadic, family, and group support formats; caregiver self-care, patient-caregiving, and family-care components; and outcome domains such as psycho-emotional, social, daily functioning, physical, spiritual, and quality-of-life outcomes.

## Supported Claims or Knowledge Roles

- Supportive interventions for advanced cancer caregivers vary by target population, content, theory, provider, delivery mode, duration, dose, and setting.
- Most included intervention studies reported at least one beneficial caregiver outcome, especially psycho-emotional outcomes.
- Individual caregiver interventions mainly aligned with psycho-emotional outcomes, while dyadic/family interventions also aligned with social outcomes and group interventions mainly aligned with daily functioning outcomes.
- Caregiver self-care was the most common intervention component in the reviewed literature.
- Intervention design should match caregiver needs, context, preferences, outcome dimension, and organizational capacity.

## Evidence Limits

This is broad advanced cancer caregiver evidence, not gynecological-cancer-specific evidence. Included studies were heterogeneous, most were conducted in Western-oriented countries, and table extraction from the two-column PDF was artifact-prone. Do not treat this as AI, smart-home, mHealth effectiveness, cancer-treatment, or autonomous care evidence.
