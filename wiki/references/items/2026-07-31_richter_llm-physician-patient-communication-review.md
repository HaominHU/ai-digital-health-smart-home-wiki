## Why This Source Matters

This source supports a narrow evidence claim about perceived empathy in selected written-response studies while documenting major gaps in safety, trust, and real-world outcomes.

## Supported Claims or Knowledge Roles

- AI responses were often rated more empathic in early written-response studies.
- Perceived empathy is distinct from authentic empathy, factual accuracy, safety, trust, and clinical benefit.
- Empathic tone can coexist with subtle harmful error, supporting clinician review and explicit governance.

## Evidence Limits

Ten early, predominantly cross-sectional studies; no randomized or longitudinal trials, no real relationship outcomes, and substantial heterogeneity in the four-study meta-analysis.

## Privacy or Sensitivity Notes

No participant-level data should be copied. Any downstream use must preserve clinician oversight and avoid autonomous health communication.
