## Why This Source Matters

This updated systematic review anchors modern digital health intervention evidence for family caregivers and adds a human-centered design, usability, accessibility, and equity lens. It is especially useful as a review-level taxonomy source for web resources, telemedicine, mHealth, intervention purposes, caregiver outcomes, and HCD gaps.

## Supported Claims or Knowledge Roles

- Digital health interventions can support caregiver psychological health, self-efficacy, caregiving skills, quality of life, social support, and coping in the reviewed literature.
- Modern caregiver digital health interventions in this review clustered around web resources, telemedicine, and mHealth, with limited text-messaging and wearable examples.
- Intervention purposes included education, real-time communication, data collection/monitoring, psychotherapy, and connection/support.
- HCD methods were present in fewer than half of included studies and often focused on usability and satisfaction.
- Future digital caregiver interventions should include marginalized caregivers, accessibility and usability work, cultural and linguistic tailoring, and caregiver psychosocial assessment.

## Evidence Limits

The review is heterogeneous and includes multiple study designs and technologies. Do not treat usability, satisfaction, feasibility, or acceptability as effectiveness evidence. Do not treat this source as AI-agent or smart-home effectiveness evidence.
