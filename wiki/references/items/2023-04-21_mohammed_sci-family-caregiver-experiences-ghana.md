## Why This Source Matters

This source provides condition-specific qualitative evidence about family caregiving for people with spinal cord injury in Ghana. It grounds SCI caregiver needs in lived experience, including sudden caregiving entry, ADL/IADL support, physical and financial burden, coping strategies, and the need for training, counselling, and social support.

## Supported Claims or Knowledge Roles

- Family caregivers of people with SCI may enter caregiving suddenly and feel unprepared.
- SCI caregiving can include hands-on ADL support, bowel/bladder support, transportation, finance management, and household/business task substitution.
- Caregivers in this Ghanaian hospital context reported physical burden, sleep disruption, tiredness, body pain, and financial burden.
- Coping strategies included religious faith, hope, social support, acceptance, and practical family support.
- Training, counselling, and social support services are recommended by the authors.

## Writing Roles

## Evidence Limits

This is a small qualitative study of 10 family caregivers at one Ghanaian hospital. It should not be used for prevalence estimates or generalized without context. It does not evaluate an intervention.
