## Evidence Limits

- Benefits are hypothetical. No measured security gain, health literacy gain, trust increase, caregiver benefit, latency, fault tolerance, or emergency response validity is established.
- Jurisdiction-specific regulatory requirements, legacy-system integration, key management, cross-provider trust, and formal compliance auditing remain unresolved. Do not describe the framework as HIPAA- or GDPR-compliant.
- The fall-alert and clinician-redirection scenarios are proposed workflows, not validated escalation systems. They do not authorize autonomous emergency dispatch, diagnosis, treatment selection, or clinical triage.
- The paper proposes future machine learning; it is not an evaluated AI intervention. Family roles are conceptual and do not measure caregiver preferences, consent, or workload.

## Privacy or Sensitivity Notes

Use published aggregate findings and generalized themes. Keep health and household data permissioned and auditable; publication does not establish consent for a new deployment or prove regulatory compliance.
