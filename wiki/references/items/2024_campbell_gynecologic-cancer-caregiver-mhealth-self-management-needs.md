## Why This Source Matters

This focus group study provides direct gynecologic cancer patient-caregiver design evidence for mHealth-supported self-management. It is especially useful for curated information needs, symptom-urgency self-triage, trajectory-sensitive support, peer/clinical expert interaction, private spaces, and granular information-sharing controls.

## Supported Claims or Knowledge Roles

- Gynecologic cancer patients and caregivers reported time-consuming searches for relevant, trustworthy information.
- Patients and caregivers wanted help determining urgency of problems and whom to contact.
- Participants wanted centralized, curated, plain-language, gynecologic oncology-specific information.
- Patients and caregivers wanted private spaces and control over what information is shared with each other and with family members.
- Formative mHealth design should support individual, dyadic, and family-sharing modes rather than assuming universal sharing.

## Evidence Limits

This is formative qualitative design evidence, not mHealth effectiveness evidence. The sample was mostly White and recruited through a trusted gynecologic oncology practice. COVID-19 timing and mixed patient-caregiver groups may have shaped responses. Do not treat this as AI, smart-home, autonomous triage, clinical decision-support, or cancer-treatment evidence.
