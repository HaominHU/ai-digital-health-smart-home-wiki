## Why This Source Matters

This article gives the wiki a reusable evaluation grammar for caregiver interventions. It separates intervention targets and mechanisms from caregiver outcomes and care-recipient outcomes, and it highlights under-measured economic and utilization outcomes.

## Supported Claims or Knowledge Roles

- Caregiver interventions should specify caregiving activities, caregiver outcomes, and care-recipient outcomes.
- Caregiving activities include clinical skills/knowledge, psychological skills/resources, support-seeking skills, and quantity of caregiving.
- Caregiver outcomes include psychological health, physical health, utilization, and economic status.
- Care-recipient outcomes include psychological health, physical health/disease management, utilization, and economic status.
- Intervention studies should assess quantity and/or quality of care, broader caregiver and care-recipient outcomes, and a common outcome set to support comparison.

## Evidence Limits

The review classified measured activities and outcomes in U.S. caregiver intervention reports from 2000 through August 31, 2009. It did not evaluate intervention effectiveness or study quality.
