## Why This Source Matters

Adds evidence about literacy heterogeneity within an already device-using chronic-disease population. It connects the wiki's access/use distinctions to hypotheses about assessment and tailoring, while exposing the selection gap created by excluding nonusers.

## Supported Claims or Knowledge Roles

- Existing device users may differ in reported information acquisition/evaluation, interaction, and application abilities.
- Low/middle/high classes were sample-derived, and most-likely-class regression has uncertainty and external-validation limits.
- Tailored education and support are proposed implications, not tested interventions or demonstrated improvements in self-management.
- Direct caregiver outcomes, family support effects, smart-home benefit, and AI effectiveness were not established.

## Evidence Limits

Cross-sectional, convenience-sampled, single-region findings cannot establish causality or universal profiles. Nonusers and people unable to participate because of substantial sensory or illness-related limitations were excluded. Reported abilities, often collected with researcher assistance, are distinct from observed performance. The paper does not evaluate self-management or caregiver benefit.

The evidence page owns the detailed reporting caveats: profile count/percentage inconsistencies; incomplete/inconsistent living-arrangement counts; a rural middle-versus-low contrast conflicting with the results narrative; overextended CHD-duration prose; and an imprecise information-seeking OR with a very wide interval. Verify the final version or obtain clarification before precision-dependent reuse. Do not silently repair estimates or treat the profiles as validated routing rules.

## Privacy or Sensitivity Notes

Published aggregate results only. New deployments would require consent, minimum necessary access, secure storage/retention, auditability, and human oversight. Living arrangements do not authorize family access or establish caregiver support duties.
