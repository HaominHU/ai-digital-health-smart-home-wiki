## Why This Source Matters

This systematic review anchors telehealth intervention evidence for family caregivers of people with chronic health conditions, with an RCT-focused evidence base and clear modality, intervention component, user-control, caregiver-application, and outcome categories.

## Supported Claims or Knowledge Roles

- Telehealth can deliver caregiver interventions across telephone, web, and combined modalities.
- Telephone was the most common delivery mode in the reviewed RCT evidence base.
- Skills training and psychoeducation are common telehealth caregiver intervention strategies.
- Psychological functioning is the most frequent caregiver outcome improvement domain.
- Intervention components included skills training, psychoeducation/resource materials, self-monitoring/tracking, reminders, and counseling.
- More evidence is needed on caregiver skills, self-efficacy, caregiver health outcomes, rural and underserved caregivers, and comparisons with in-person delivery.

## Evidence Limits

The review is broad and heterogeneous and did not conduct a meta-analysis. It should not be treated as AI-agent, smart-home, or care-recipient clinical effectiveness evidence.
