## Why This Source Matters

This source provides a systematic review anchor for SCI caregiving service structure: formal and informal care, care hours, functional predictors of care need, training, quality-of-care gaps, and intervention research gaps.

## Supported Claims or Knowledge Roles

- SCI caregiving services are predominantly provided by informal caregivers, often women.
- Care hours vary widely and are associated with injury severity, functional independence, and IADL capacity.
- Caregiver or attendant training has preliminary evidence for improving knowledge about secondary health conditions and health maintenance.
- SCI caregiving service intervention evidence is limited and generally low quality.
- Caregiver burden and lived experience need separate evidence sources because this review excluded those studies.

## Evidence Limits

Most included studies were low-level evidence. The review excluded caregiver burden and lived-experience studies and does not establish strong intervention effectiveness, AI evidence, smart-home evidence, mHealth evidence, or disease-background knowledge.
