## Why This Source Matters

This narrative review provides broad cancer caregiver unmet-needs and advocacy-ecosystem framing. It is useful for organizing caregiver needs into informational, relationship/communication, emotional, and practical/financial domains and for identifying patient advocacy groups as resource, navigation, counseling, financial-aid, and policy-advocacy actors.

## Supported Claims or Knowledge Roles

- Cancer caregivers may perform medical, logistical, advocacy, and decision-support work without adequate training or recognition.
- Broad cancer caregiver needs can be grouped into informational, relationship/communication, emotional, and practical/financial domains.
- Telehealth can help caregivers participate in visits but may introduce access, literacy, device, portal, geography, and reimbursement barriers.
- Patient advocacy groups can provide education, resources, counseling, navigation, financial assistance, and policy advocacy.
- Advocacy-group linkage is a design and referral rationale, not outcome-effectiveness proof by itself.

## Evidence Limits

This is a narrative review with author expertise and selected sources, not a systematic review. It includes authors affiliated with Pfizer and patient advocacy groups. Do not treat it as gynecological-cancer-specific evidence, intervention-effectiveness proof, AI evidence, smart-home evidence, mHealth effectiveness evidence, or cancer-treatment evidence.
