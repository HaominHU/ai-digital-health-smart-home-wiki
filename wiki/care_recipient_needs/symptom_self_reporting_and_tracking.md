# Symptom Self-Reporting and Tracking

## Need Summary

Care recipients may need accessible tools for reporting symptoms, severity, duration, frequency, and contextual factors without excessive burden or avoidable data loss.

## Relevant Populations

- People with chronic conditions and disabilities.
- People with systemic sclerosis-associated Raynaud phenomenon.

## Relevant Conditions

- Systemic sclerosis-associated Raynaud phenomenon.

## Evidence

- `2026-05-18_hu_raynaud-phenomenon-mhealth-usability`: The Raynaud app supported real-time capture of Raynaud attack frequency, duration, severity, and contextual factors. The abstract frames this as important for clinical research and contrasts app-based capture with paper diaries that can involve reporting fatigue, recall bias, and low adherence.
- `2019-04-25_setiawan_adaptive-mhealth-self-management`: iMHere 2.0 supports broader self-management tracking across medication, bowel management, bladder self-catheterization, mood, skin, exercise, nutrition, education, goals, personal health records, and messaging.

## Design Implications

- Minimize multi-step entry burden where possible.
- Support users with dexterity limitations, lower technology literacy, and accessibility needs.
- Design for real-world reliability across indoor-outdoor transitions and unstable network conditions.

## Gaps

- The source reports usability and refinement, not clinical effectiveness.
- Caregiver involvement is not a primary focus of this source.
