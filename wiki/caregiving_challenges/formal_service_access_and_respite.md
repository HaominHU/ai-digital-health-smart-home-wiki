# Formal Service Access and Respite

## Challenge Summary

Caregivers may need help obtaining human services, practical assistance, and time away from care. Knowing about a service, expressing interest, searching, receiving a referral, qualifying, and actually receiving suitable support are different states. This hub owns those access distinctions; shared-access and health-literacy pages retain their separate coordination and information roles.

## Evidence-Backed Anchors

- Chamberlin's survey of 511 caregivers of older US veterans found similar rural/urban service interest. Among the 226 service searchers, rural receipt of caregiver support, community services, and helping services was lower. Rural searchers were more likely to seek respite, but respite receipt did not differ significantly. The study did not link a specific search to later receipt or test a referral intervention. See `wiki/evidence/chamberlin_2026_rural_urban_caregiver_service_access.md`.
- Smith's SCI review maps formal/informal care, care hours, functional predictors, and training needs. It excluded caregiver burden studies and largely contains low-level evidence, so it supports service structure rather than respite effectiveness. See `wiki/evidence/smith_2016_caregiving_services_sci_systematic_review.md`.
- Ryerson Espino's SCI dyad study describes supplemental-care, respite, compensation, insurance, and navigation gaps alongside caregiver well-being. Exploratory profiles are not validated triage categories. See `wiki/evidence/espino_2022_coping_social_support_caregiver_wellbeing_sci.md`.

## Caregiver and Care-Recipient Needs

Caregiver needs include relief from care work, backup arrangements, navigation support, and sustainable access to help. Care-recipient needs include acceptable, competent, continuous support that respects preferences, independence, and consent. Service receipt for one member of a dyad does not automatically improve the other's outcomes.

## Design Implications and Measures

These are inferred research and implementation requirements, not proven service interventions:

- Track awareness, interest, search, referral, eligibility, wait time, receipt, unmet need, and continued use separately, with dates and denominators when evaluating a pathway.
- Identify the reason an access attempt fails: unavailable service, staffing, distance, transport, cost, eligibility, language, accessibility, preference, or incomplete follow-up. Chamberlin does not establish which of these caused the rural differences.
- Pair directories with optional human navigation, offline contact, and confirmation of service receipt; a click or referral sent is not completed access.
- Evaluate respite availability, uptake, suitability, caregiver time actually released, and care continuity without assuming universal burden reduction.
- Keep service workforce, funding, and geographic availability visible. An app cannot be assumed to substitute for missing local capacity.
- Preserve veteran/VA and SCI context when transferring findings to other populations; do not infer that rural caregivers have less interest or lower motivation.

## Privacy and Decision Boundary

Collect only the information required for navigation. Confirm consent before referrals or information sharing, restrict access, record revocation and retention rules, and avoid storing unnecessary medical or financial details. Eligibility information must be current and reviewed by the relevant service; this wiki does not determine benefits, clinical urgency, or treatment. Urgent situations require established human escalation routes, not routine directory workflows.

## Knowledge Gaps

- Causal and longitudinal evidence linking access barriers, completed referrals, and caregiver or care-recipient outcomes.
- Evaluated digital-plus-human navigation and respite models across rural and non-veteran populations.
- Workforce, cost, service-quality, language-access, and maintenance evidence beyond initial receipt.
