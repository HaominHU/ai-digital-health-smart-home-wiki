# Family Caregivers

## Scope

Primary population focus for this wiki.

## Needs to Track

- Care coordination.
- Monitoring and safety awareness.
- Emotional and cognitive burden.
- Training and self-efficacy.
- Communication with care recipients and professionals.
- Respite and sustainability.
- Technology burden and usability.
- Time restrictions that limit self-directed app exploration.
- Health literacy variation and plain-language support needs.
- Evolving informational needs across the caregiving trajectory.
- Emotional check-ins, peer connection, and situation-specific contact support.
- Self-care and health tracking needs that can be crowded out by caregiving duties.
- Training and reliable condition-specific education when entering caregiving roles with limited preparation.
- Care coordination, shared access, reminders, and public resource navigation.
- Older-adult caregiving trajectories that accumulate household, self-care, medical, emotional, coordination, advocacy, and surrogate decision-making tasks.
- Caregiver identification, assessment, training, and referral needs in health care and long-term services and supports.
- Intervention evaluation needs that separate caregiving activities from caregiver outcomes and care-recipient outcomes.
- Reach, adoption, implementation, and maintenance risks when caregiver interventions rely on caregivers having time, technology access, literacy, clinic linkage, or sustained engagement capacity.

## Evidence

- `2026-05-18_hu_hcd-generative-ai-family-caregiver-mhealth`: An AMIA abstract on human-centered design for generative AI in caregiver mHealth reported that family caregivers face time restrictions, need intuitive navigation and just-in-time prompts, may need plain-language condition-specific education, and have evolving informational, emotional, and social support needs.
- `2026-05-18_hu_dissertation-family-caregiver-mhealth-app`: Hu's dissertation developed and evaluated a multi-component mHealth app for family caregivers of people with chronic conditions and disabilities. Across focus group, co-design, and pilot phases, it identified needs around caregiver self-care, training, monitoring, reminders, care coordination, accessible information, emotional support, public resources, and adaptive mHealth design.
- `2020_schulz_family-caregiving-for-older-adults`: Schulz et al. 2020 provides a broad older-adult caregiving anchor, framing caregiving as chronic stress exposure and summarizing cumulative role complexity, health impacts, risk factors, and mixed intervention evidence.
- `2016_nasem_families-caring-for-an-aging-america`: The National Academies report defines family caregivers broadly, estimates 17.7 million U.S. caregivers of older adults in 2011 outside nursing home settings, and calls for caregiver identification, assessment, support, and person- and family-centered care.
- `2011-11-22_van-houtven_organizing-framework-informal-caregiver-interventions`: Van Houtven et al. 2011 supports separating caregiving activities from caregiver outcomes and care-recipient outcomes when evaluating caregiver interventions.
- `2023-04-21_mohammed_sci-family-caregiver-experiences-ghana`: A Ghanaian qualitative study provides SCI-specific evidence that family caregivers may enter caregiving suddenly, perform ADL/IADL and inpatient hands-on care tasks, and experience physical and financial burden while using social, religious, and family coping supports.
- `2016-04_hartnett_caregiver-burden-end-stage-ovarian-cancer`: A pilot study of end-stage ovarian cancer caregivers found that positive caregiver esteem can coexist with disrupted schedules and financial burden, supporting separate tracking of positive meaning and practical burden.
- `2001_glasgow_re-aim-framework-chronic-illness-management`: RE-AIM is not caregiver-specific evidence, but it supports evaluating caregiver interventions for representativeness, adoption, implementation consistency, and sustained use.
