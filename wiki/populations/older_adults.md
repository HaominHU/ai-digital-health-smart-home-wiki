# Older Adults

## Scope

Secondary population focus for this wiki, with strong relevance to family caregiving.

## Conceptual Caution

Do not treat diseases common in later life as normal aging.

Caregiving need among older adults should be linked to functional limitation, cognitive impairment, mental health, chronic disease, disability, and care complexity rather than age alone.

## Needs to Track

- Functional ability.
- Safety and fall risk.
- Autonomy and dignity.
- Home environment fit.
- Cognitive, sensory, mobility, and communication needs.
- Chronic disease and multimorbidity context.
- Font size, contrast, and interaction accessibility in digital health tools.
- Reach and access barriers in intervention delivery, including technology access, transportation, scheduling, referrals, and sustained engagement.

## Evidence

- `2026-05-18_hu_raynaud-phenomenon-mhealth-usability`: In a usability abstract about a Raynaud mHealth app for people with systemic sclerosis-associated Raynaud phenomenon, older adults flagged small font sizes and low contrast as barriers. This is an accessibility/usability note, not evidence that SSc-RP is an aging issue.
- `2008_schulz-sherwood_physical-mental-health-effects-family-caregiving`: Schulz and Sherwood 2008 argues that caregivers should be integrated into planning and delivery of health care for older adults, while caregiver health effects should be tracked separately from care-recipient needs.
- `2021_graven_telehealth-interventions-family-caregivers-chronic-conditions`: Many reviewed telehealth RCTs involved dementia and stroke caregivers, making the source relevant to older-adult caregiving contexts when need is tied to disease, disability, or functional/cognitive impairment.
- `2023_zhai_digital-health-interventions-support-family-caregivers`: Digital health caregiver interventions are relevant to older-adult care when tied to dementia, late-life disease, disability, or care complexity, not age alone.
- `2016_nasem_families-caring-for-an-aging-america`: The National Academies report explicitly states that need for caregiving is not an inevitable consequence of aging; functional, cognitive, mental health, and chronic health limitations drive the need for help.
- `2020_schulz_family-caregiving-for-older-adults`: Schulz et al. 2020 anchors older-adult caregiving in chronic illness, disability, care intensity, dementia, multimorbidity, and health/social system context rather than normal aging alone.
- `2001_glasgow_re-aim-framework-chronic-illness-management`: RE-AIM supports evaluating whether chronic illness and caregiver interventions reach older adults equitably and can be adopted, implemented, and maintained in real-world settings.
- `2025_hasan_carebuddy-multi-agent-conversational-ai-alzheimers`: The abstract included older adults with and without early-stage memory impairment in a usability study of a multi-agent conversational AI; treat this as usability context, not evidence that dementia is normal aging.
- `2025-12-30_malhotra_carebuddy-mobile-care-ecosystem-dementia-caregiving`: The dementia caregiver app context is relevant to older adults when dementia-related cognitive impairment and care needs are present, not age alone.
