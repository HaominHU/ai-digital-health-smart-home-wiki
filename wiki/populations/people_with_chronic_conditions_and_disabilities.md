# People with Chronic Conditions and Disabilities

## Scope

Secondary population focus for this wiki, especially when their needs interact with family caregiving and technology support.

## Conceptual Caution

Do not collapse disability, chronic disease, and aging-related decline into one category.

## Needs to Track

- Functional support.
- Autonomy.
- Symptom or condition management.
- Accessibility.
- Privacy and dignity.
- Care coordination.
- Technology fit and burden.
- Dexterity-related interaction barriers.
- Technology-literacy barriers and fear of making mistakes during app use.
- Reliability of symptom-reporting tools during real-world context changes.
- Condition-specific caregiving content that can be delivered through reusable app interfaces without collapsing distinct conditions into one category.

## Evidence

- `2026-05-18_hu_hcd-generative-ai-family-caregiver-mhealth`: An AMIA abstract on caregiver mHealth framed family caregiver support around people with chronic conditions and disabilities, emphasizing that support needs can be broad, evolving, and not well served by static or single-condition app designs.
- `2026-05-18_hu_raynaud-phenomenon-mhealth-usability`: An AMIA abstract on a Raynaud mHealth app found that symptom-reporting tools for people with systemic sclerosis-associated Raynaud phenomenon need to account for dexterity limitations, accessibility barriers, technology literacy, network instability, and cache staleness.
- `2026-05-18_hu_dissertation-family-caregiver-mhealth-app`: The dissertation frames people with chronic conditions and disabilities as the care-recipient population whose functional, medical, and condition-specific needs shape family caregiver support requirements.
- `2019-04-25_setiawan_adaptive-mhealth-self-management`: Published JMIR evidence describes iMHere 2.0 as an adaptive mHealth system for people with chronic conditions and disabilities whose self-management needs are diverse, change over time, and require long-term engagement support.
