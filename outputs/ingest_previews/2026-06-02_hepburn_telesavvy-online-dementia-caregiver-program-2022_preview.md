## Evidence-Backed Extraction

This randomized trial tested Tele-Savvy, a synchronous/asynchronous online adaptation of the in-person Savvy Caregiver program.

Full-study picture:

- Problem/gap: Dementia caregiver interventions have shown benefits, but in-person attendance is difficult for many caregivers. Distance delivery may improve access while retaining active caregiver role training.
- Population/condition/setting: Family caregivers of community-dwelling persons living with Alzheimer disease and related dementias; study conducted through four NIA Alzheimer Disease Centers.
- Intervention/system: Tele-Savvy was delivered over 43 days to small caregiver groups through seven weekly synchronous Zoom sessions plus 36 brief asynchronous video lessons.
- Comparator: Three-arm waitlist randomized design with active Tele-Savvy, attention-control Healthy Living program, and usual care.
- Key innovation: Online program preserved Savvy's active-learning and mastery-building curriculum using synchronous coaching/debriefing and asynchronous lessons.
- Methods/sample/measures: 23 cohorts, 261 caregivers enrolled; 2:2:1 randomization. Outcomes measured caregiver psychological well-being and caregiving mastery at baseline and follow-ups. Main analysis used baseline-to-6-month data because COVID-19 restrictions were expected to confound later psychological outcomes.
- Key results: Tele-Savvy showed statistically and clinically significant benefits for depression, perceived stress, caregiver reaction to care-recipient behaviors, and caregiver mastery. Expected benefits for caregiver burden and anxiety were not found.
- Primary takeaway: Online synchronous/asynchronous psychoeducation can improve dementia caregiver emotional well-being and mastery over 6 months, but access, scheduling, connectivity, representation, and burden outcomes remain important limits.
- Evidence limits: Trial was truncated due to COVID-19 context; participation required technology access, English comprehension, and schedule fit; Hispanic and Asian caregiver representation was limited; caregiver burden and anxiety effects were not statistically significant.

## Gaps and Cautions

- Do not treat Tele-Savvy as AI-agent or smart-home evidence.
- Do not convert depression/stress/mastery effects into claims about reduced clinical dementia progression.
- Do not ignore the access constraint: participation required internet-connected technology and time availability for synchronous groups.
- Usability/access feasibility is not the same as caregiver outcome effectiveness; this paper supports some outcome effectiveness but also exposes reach limits.
