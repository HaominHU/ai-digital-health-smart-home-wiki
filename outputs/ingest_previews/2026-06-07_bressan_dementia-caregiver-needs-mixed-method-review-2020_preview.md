## Evidence-Backed Extraction

- Review question: While caring for a loved one living with dementia at home, what needs of family caregivers have been reported in the literature?
- Inclusion logic: Included English quantitative, qualitative, and mixed-method primary studies from 2009-2019 that investigated needs of adult family caregivers of people with dementia living at home. Excluded caregiver needs for other conditions, care-recipient-only needs, nursing-home settings, professional/formal caregivers, underage caregivers, dissertations, commentaries, letters, editorials, and secondary reviews.
- Search/screening: PubMed, CINAHL, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, and PsycINFO; 1,376 citations identified, 1,196 screened after duplicates, 105 full texts assessed, 34 studies included.
- Quality appraisal: Used the Mixed-Methods Appraisal Tool. Study quality was assessed to guide interpretation rather than as an exclusion criterion.
- Population/setting scope: Family caregivers of home-dwelling people with dementia; most included caregivers were women, with many studies from the United States and other high-income settings.
- Needs taxonomy: Four interrelated themes: being supported; receiving accessible and personalized information; being trained and educated to manage changes; and finding a balance.
- Support needs: Caregivers needed social support, formal services, daily care assistance, dementia-specific services, respite, financial/legal assistance, psychological support, emotional support, and access to medical or mental healthcare support.
- Information/training needs: Caregivers needed timely, understandable, trusted, tailored information about dementia diagnosis, symptoms, trajectory, medications, side effects, behavioral issues, emergencies, services, financial support, legal concerns, home adaptation, incontinence, nutrition, communication, and nonpharmacological strategies.
- Balance/sustainability needs: Caregivers needed time for themselves, health protection, social life, respite, stability/control, acceptance of help, coping with changed roles, and future planning.
- Review-level takeaway: Dementia caregiver needs are dynamic and trajectory-sensitive; support design should treat caregiver and care-recipient needs as intersecting but not identical, and should adapt as disease progression changes tasks, stressors, service needs, and personal-life constraints.
- What the review does not answer: It does not test intervention effectiveness, compare digital versus nondigital support, establish AI/chatbot value, or provide condition-general caregiver needs beyond home-based dementia caregiving.

## Review-Level Takeaway and Story

### Why this review exists

Dementia caregiver support is often discussed through burden or distress, but caregivers also need concrete information, training, services, respite, legal/financial guidance, emotional support, and room for their own life. Bressan et al. synthesize studies that directly or indirectly identify what family caregivers of people with dementia need at home.

### What the review is really about

The review is a needs architecture for home-based dementia caregiving. Its value is not an intervention effect estimate; it shows how caregiver needs cluster and change as dementia symptoms, family roles, care tasks, and service needs evolve.

### What it lets the wiki say

- Dementia caregiver needs should be modeled as dynamic and trajectory-sensitive.
- Four high-level needs should be preserved: being supported, receiving accessible and personalized information, being trained and educated to manage changes, and finding a balance.
- Caregiver support should include service navigation, emotional support, training, respite, legal/financial help, future planning, and self-care sustainability.
- Caregiver and care-recipient needs intersect, but they are not the same analytic object.

### What it does not let the wiki say

- It does not show that meeting these needs through any particular intervention improves outcomes.
- It does not establish digital, AI, chatbot, or smart-home effectiveness.
- It does not generalize automatically beyond home-based dementia caregiving.
- It does not justify treating dementia as normal aging.

### Design interpretation

For caregiver-system design, Bressan 2020 should be the dementia needs backbone. A digital or AI caregiver system should not start with a chatbot feature; it should start by asking which trajectory-sensitive need is present now: support, information, training, balance, respite, service navigation, legal/financial planning, or escalation to human help.

## Gaps and Cautions

- Do not treat dementia as normal aging.
- Do not treat caregiver needs as care-recipient needs, even where they intersect.
- Do not treat needs synthesis as intervention-effectiveness evidence.
- Generalization to men, younger caregivers, low-resource contexts, and non-English evidence is limited.
