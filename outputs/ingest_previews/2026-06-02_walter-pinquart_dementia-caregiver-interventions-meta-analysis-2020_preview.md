## Evidence-Backed Extraction

This meta-analysis updates Pinquart and Sorensen's 2006 synthesis of dementia caregiver intervention effects.

Review-level extraction:

- Review question: What average effects do dementia caregiver interventions have on caregiver burden, depression, anxiety, subjective well-being, ability/knowledge, care-recipient symptoms, and institutionalization, and how do effects differ by intervention type and study characteristics?
- Inclusion logic: Included controlled studies where the care recipient had dementia, participants were informal caregivers, the psychosocial intervention targeted caregiver experience or behavior, assignment avoided systematic pre-treatment differences, and at least one desired outcome allowed effect-size computation.
- Search/screening: PubMed, PsychINFO, and PSYNDEX search focused on post-2006 literature, combined with the 2006 meta-analysis base. The source reports 431 full-text articles assessed and 280 articles included in quantitative synthesis, producing 332 intervention comparisons.
- Intervention taxonomy: Psychoeducation, CBT, counseling/case management, general support, respite, care-recipient training, multicomponent interventions, and miscellaneous interventions.
- Outcome taxonomy: Caregiver burden, depression, anxiety, subjective well-being, ability/knowledge, care-recipient symptoms, and institutionalization.
- Main results: Aggregate effects were significant and generally small-to-moderate for caregiver burden, depression, anxiety, subjective well-being, ability/knowledge, and care-recipient symptoms at post-test and follow-up. No mean effect was found for institutionalization overall.
- Intervention-type takeaway: Psychoeducation and multicomponent interventions affected the most outcomes; psychoeducation worked broadly when it required active caregiver participation, while information-only psychoeducation mainly improved knowledge. Only multicomponent interventions reduced institutionalization risk at post-test.
- Evidence quality/heterogeneity: Significant heterogeneity was present across outcomes. Funnel plot asymmetry suggested possible publication bias or study-characteristic imbalance. Coding intervention type was sometimes ambiguous because intervention components overlapped.
- Review-level takeaway: Dementia caregiver interventions have real but mostly modest average effects; intervention selection should be matched to the desired outcome, and active training matters more than information provision alone.
- What the review does not answer: It does not identify the best specific intervention for a given caregiver profile, isolate individual active components, prove long-term institutionalization reduction across intervention types, or establish AI/smart-home intervention effectiveness.

## Gaps and Cautions

- Do not treat average small-to-moderate effects as strong effects for every intervention or every caregiver group.
- Do not treat institutionalization findings as broad proof; overall mean institutionalization effects were not significant and multicomponent post-test effects require careful interpretation.
- Do not treat caregiver intervention evidence as dementia clinical treatment evidence.
- Do not use this source as AI, mHealth, or smart-home evidence except as a general caregiver-intervention outcome anchor.
- Effects are heterogeneous; intervention type, active participation, caregiver age/gender/relationship, sample size, and publication bias concerns matter.
