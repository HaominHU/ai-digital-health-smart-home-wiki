## Evidence-Backed Extraction

This article examines healthcare costs associated with dementia caregiver participation in REACH II and REACH VA, both behavioral caregiver interventions intended to improve caregiver coping and care-recipient management.

Full-study picture:

- Problem/gap: Prior REACH work showed dementia caregiver benefits, but healthcare systems may hesitate to adopt caregiver interventions if they appear likely to increase patient or caregiver healthcare costs.
- Population/condition/setting: Caregivers and care recipients with Alzheimer disease and related dementias. REACH II drew from five community sites; REACH VA drew from 24 VA facilities and Home Based Primary Care context.
- Intervention/phenomenon: REACH II was an RCT caregiver behavioral intervention; REACH VA was a VA translation implemented as usual clinical care with a matched comparison group.
- Methods/sample/measures: REACH II cost analysis used Medicare expenditures for an analysis sample of 110 caregivers and 197 care recipients. REACH VA used VA costs plus Medicare expenditures for 491 care recipients in an intervention-versus-propensity-matched comparison.
- Key results: Neither REACH II nor REACH VA was associated with increased VA or Medicare expenditures for care recipients or caregivers where data were available. REACH VA was associated with 33.6% lower total VA care costs for care recipients; VA caregiver cost data were not available.
- Primary takeaway: Dementia caregiver behavioral interventions can be framed as support mechanisms that may avoid additional healthcare costs, and in an integrated VA setting may be associated with lower care-recipient costs.
- Evidence limits: REACH VA was not randomized for cost comparison, caregiver cost data were unavailable for VA caregivers, and the cost finding should not be treated as proof that all caregiver interventions reduce costs.

## Gaps and Cautions

- Do not treat this as evidence that REACH improves dementia symptoms or slows dementia progression.
- Do not generalize REACH VA cost savings outside integrated care systems without additional evidence.
- Do not use cost non-increase as a substitute for caregiver outcome effectiveness.
- VA caregiver costs were unavailable, limiting caregiver economic conclusions.
