## Evidence-Backed Extraction

This article reports development and field testing of the Savvy Caregiver Program as a transportable dementia caregiver training program derived from an academic psychoeducational intervention.

Full-study picture:

- Problem/gap: Research-based caregiver psychoeducation programs can help caregivers, but broad community benefit requires interventions that can be transported to other sites without the original expert team.
- Population/condition/setting: Dementia family caregivers in field tests across rural Minnesota, Colorado, and Alaska; 140 family caregivers participated in expanded field testing.
- Intervention/system: A 12-hour, six-session self-contained caregiver training program with trainer manual, caregiver manual, structured curriculum, homework, and cognitive-performance estimation support.
- Key innovation: Codified caregiver role training designed to preserve program integrity while allowing adoption by varied community, health, government, and social service organizations.
- Conceptual content: Caregiving is framed as a new role requiring disease acknowledgment, cognitive shift, emotional tolerance, taking control when dementia erodes autonomy, realistic care goals, capability estimation, activity design, and problem solving.
- Methods/sample/measures: Formative development, local field tests, expanded field tests, participant evaluations, and pre-post caregiver measures including behavior-reaction, Zarit burden, CES-D depression, mastery, and caregiving beliefs.
- Key results: Participants reported increased knowledge, skills, and confidence and all respondents would recommend the program. Pre-post analyses showed significant improvement in total reaction to care-recipient behavior, Zarit burden, and beliefs about caregiving; depression and mastery did not significantly change in the reported field-test measures.
- Primary takeaway: Savvy supports implementation design for manualized, role-focused dementia caregiver training and transportability, but this article is not a randomized effectiveness trial.
- Evidence limits: Field-test/pre-post design, no randomized control group in this report, limited response follow-up, sample was mostly non-Hispanic White and relatively well educated/affluent.

## Gaps and Cautions

- Do not treat this field-test article as RCT-level effectiveness evidence.
- Do not generalize field-test acceptability to underserved, low-literacy, non-English-speaking, or digitally constrained caregivers.
- The program's stance on taking control must be handled carefully in the wiki alongside autonomy, dignity, and dementia-specific decision-capacity concerns.
- This is dementia caregiver training evidence, not evidence that dementia is normal aging.
